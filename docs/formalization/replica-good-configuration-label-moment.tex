\section{Actual signed physical moments on the full good-copy density}

The three physical tensor factors are $Q,Y,V$, with $Y$ retained as its own
factor. The vector $\Omega$ may be entangled among these factors.
The two auxiliary factors are $C,R$. Let $\Omega$ be a unit ground vector,
let $u$ be an arbitrary original vector, and let $w=(R_B\otimes I)u$ be its
actual excitation component. The density $\rho_G$ retains every good copy of
all five factors and traces only bad copies. Thus no good middle physical
coordinate has been discarded.

For a physical factor $A$, take its actual one-copy marginal
$\sigma_A=\operatorname{Tr}_{A^c}|\Omega\rangle\langle\Omega|$ and put
$S_A=S(\sigma_A)$. The only moment hypothesis is the original one-copy
surprisal-growth bound from the manuscript. For every $s$ with
$|s|\leq c_0/\sqrt{\mathcal B}$, it states
\begin{align}
 \log\sum_j p_j e^{-s\log p_j}
 \leq sS_A+C_0\mathcal B s^2,
\end{align}
where $p_j$ are the positive eigenvalues of this marginal, $C_0\geq0$,
$c_0>0$, and $\mathcal B\geq1$.
Writing $m=|B^c|$ and $q_A=\dim A$, the actual singleton label observable
on the good copies satisfies, for every $t$ with
$|t|\leq\min(c_0,1)/(2\sqrt{\mathcal B})$,
\begin{align}
 \operatorname{ReTr}\!\left(\rho_G
 e^{t(\widehat F_A^{(m)}-mS_AI)}\right)
 \leq \|w\|^2
 \exp\!\left(2C_0m\mathcal B t^2+\frac{q_A^2}{2}\log(m+1)\right).
\end{align}
The estimate applies to either sign, singular ground marginals, zero
components, and zero good copies. The original vector need not be unit
or permutation invariant.

The product density of the good physical copies is first obtained from the
actual excitation component. Tracing the bad auxiliary copies preserves
its literal mass. Canonical finite-product coordinates carry the singleton
copy action and its central label sums to the selected physical factor.
The reduced pure tensor power is exactly $\sigma_A^{\otimes m}$, so the trace
pairing equals the independent-copy moment multiplied by $\|w\|^2$.
Exponentiating the independent-copy logarithmic bound finishes the estimate.
No division by the component mass occurs.
