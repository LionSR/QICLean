\begin{abstract}
This note records discrepancies in Michael M.~Wolf's \emph{Quantum Channels
\& Operations: Guided Tour} that were exposed by the present Lean
formalization or by the source-faithfulness analysis required for that
formalization \cite{Wolf2012Quantum}. It is not a general errata list. The main
body gives the formalization-derived corrections, and the appendix records
three separately audited errors in the local PDF transcription. Each entry
states the source assertion, gives the local correction, and explains why the
correction is necessary.
\end{abstract}

\section{Scope}

The source PDFs are stored in \path{Notes/WolfNotePDF/}, and the local
transcription is stored in \path{Notes/WolfNoteTexSource/}. The corresponding
formal declarations and paper-gap records are identified below. The entries
distinguish three cases:
\begin{itemize}
    \item A \emph{source error} is a printed mathematical assertion that is
          false as stated.
    \item A \emph{well-posedness correction} makes an implicit boundary
          convention explicit without changing the substantive theorem.
    \item A \emph{formalization scope gap} records that the current Lean
          theorem is narrower than the source theorem or that an auxiliary
          proof is incomplete. It is not an erratum.
\end{itemize}
Only the first two cases appear in the formalization-derived main body. The
appendix treats transcription errors separately.

\section{The trace sign in the Lorentz-cone converse}
\label{sec:lorentz}

\paragraph{Formalization trigger.}
The formal converse requires the trace to be nonnegative. Its declaration is
\leanid{Matrix.IsHermitian.posSemidef_of_trace_re_nonneg_of_card_sub_one_mul_trace_sq_re_le}
in \doclink{QICLean/Analysis/MatrixTraceInequalities}. The discrepancy is
tracked in
\path{docs/paper-gaps/wolf_ch3_lorentz_cone_trace_sign.tex}.

\paragraph{Printed assertion.}
At the end of the discussion of automorphisms of the positive cone, the first
part of Chapter~3, Proposition~3.7 gives a necessary trace inequality for a
positive semidefinite Hermitian matrix. Its converse is intended to
characterize the associated Lorentz cone. For a Hermitian matrix
$A\in M_d(\mathbb C)$, the local PDF on book p.~66 prints
\begin{align}
    \tr(A)^2
        &\geq (d-1)\tr(A^2)
        \Longrightarrow A\geq0.
    \label{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_lorentz_printed_converse}
\end{align}
This is the printed converse of Proposition~3.7
\cite[Proposition~3.7]{Wolf2012Quantum}.

\paragraph{Correction.}
The valid converse is
\begin{align}
    A=A^\dagger,\qquad
    \tr(A)\geq0,\qquad
    \tr(A)^2\geq(d-1)\tr(A^2)
        &\Longrightarrow A\geq0.
    \label{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_lorentz_corrected_converse}
\end{align}
The complex-matrix Lean statement uses real parts of the traces. For Hermitian
matrices, these real parts equal the displayed real quantities.

\paragraph{Necessity of the correction.}
Set $A=-\Id_d$. Then $A$ is Hermitian and is not positive semidefinite, but
\begin{align}
    \tr(A)^2
        &=d^2,
    \label{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_lorentz_counterexample_trace}\\
    (d-1)\tr(A^2)
        &=d(d-1),
    \label{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_lorentz_counterexample_square}\\
    \tr(A)^2
        &\geq(d-1)\tr(A^2).
    \label{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_lorentz_counterexample_inequality}
\end{align}
Thus the squared inequality describes both orientations of the Lorentz cone.
The condition $\tr(A)\geq0$ selects the positive sheet. The source proof also
uses this sign: after bounding $\tr(A)$ above by a nonnegative quantity in the
presence of a negative eigenvalue, it squares the bound
\cite[Proposition~3.7]{Wolf2012Quantum}. That step requires the trace sign.

\paragraph{Classification.}
The unrestricted converse in
Eq.~\ref{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_lorentz_printed_converse} is a source
error. The formal theorem proves the corrected statement in
Eq.~\ref{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_lorentz_corrected_converse}.

\section{The missing nonvanishing condition in Kramers' theorem~II}
\label{sec:kramers-ii}

\paragraph{Formalization trigger.}
The conditional form is formalized by
\leanid{Matrix.IsHermitian.two_le_finrank_eigenspace_of_intertwiner_mulVec_star_ne_zero},
and the invertible-intertwiner form is formalized by
\leanid{Matrix.IsHermitian.two_le_finrank_eigenspace_of_antisymmetric_isUnit}
in \doclink{QICLean/Algebra/KramersDegeneracy}. The same file recovers the
first Kramers theorem as the unitary special case. The source error is
documented in
\path{docs/paper-gaps/wolf_ch3_kramers_theorem_ii.tex}.

\paragraph{Printed assertion.}
Wolf first states that if a Hermitian matrix $H$ commutes with an anti-unitary
$T$ satisfying $T^2=-\Id$, then every eigenvalue of $H$ has multiplicity at
least two \cite{Wolf2012Quantum}. The subsequent ``Kramer's theorem II''
asserts that if
\begin{align}
    H&=H^\dagger,
    \label{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_kramers_hermitian}\\
    HA&=AH^T,
    \label{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_kramers_intertwining}\\
    A^T&=-A,\qquad A\neq0,
    \label{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_kramers_antisymmetric}
\end{align}
then every eigenvalue of $H$ has multiplicity at least two, ``as the same proof
shows'' \cite{Wolf2012Quantum}. The local source is
\path{Notes/WolfNoteTexSource/ch03_positive_not_completely.tex},
lines~527--530; the matrix reduction occurs at lines~500--501.

\paragraph{Correction.}
Let $\ket{\psi}$ be a $\lambda$-eigenvector of $H$. Under
Eq.~\ref{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_kramers_intertwining}, the vector
$A\ket{\overline\psi}$ is another $\lambda$-eigenvector. Antisymmetry makes it
orthogonal to $\ket{\psi}$. These facts imply degeneracy only if
\begin{align}
    A\ket{\overline\psi}&\neq0.
    \label{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_kramers_partner_nonzero}
\end{align}
The correct local conclusion is therefore
\begin{align}
    A\ket{\overline\psi}\neq0
        &\Longrightarrow \dim\ker(H-\lambda\Id)\geq2.
    \label{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_kramers_local_conclusion}
\end{align}
A global conclusion follows if $A$ is injective. In finite dimensions, the
corrected global theorem may assume that $A$ is invertible and then conclude
that every eigenvalue of $H$ has multiplicity at least two.

\paragraph{Necessity of the correction.}
On $\mathbb C^3$, let
\begin{align}
    H&=\operatorname{diag}(0,1,1),
    \label{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_kramers_counterexample_h}\\
    A&=
    \begin{pmatrix}
        0&0&0\\
        0&0&1\\
        0&-1&0
    \end{pmatrix}.
    \label{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_kramers_counterexample_a}
\end{align}
Then
\begin{align}
    HA&=AH^T=A,
    \label{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_kramers_counterexample_intertwining}\\
    A&\neq0,
    \label{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_kramers_counterexample_nonzero}\\
    Ae_1&=0.
    \label{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_kramers_counterexample_kernel}
\end{align}
The eigenvalue $0$ is simple, so the printed conclusion fails.

\paragraph{Classification.}
This is a source error. The first Kramers theorem remains valid because it is
the case $A=V^\dagger$ of the corrected theorem, where unitarity supplies the
missing nonvanishing condition \cite{Wolf2012Quantum}.

\section{The false converse for Pauli-block truncation}
\label{sec:pauli-block-truncation}

\paragraph{Formalization trigger.}
The forward preservation results are formalized by
\leanid{Wolf.pauliBlockDiagonalTruncation_isPositiveMap} and
\leanid{Wolf.pauliBlockDiagonalTruncation_isCPMap} in
\doclink{QICLean/Channel/LorentzNormalForm/PauliBlockTruncation}. The source
error is documented in
\path{docs/paper-gaps/wolf_prop2_10_block_truncation_converse.tex}.

\paragraph{Printed assertion.}
After the Bloch-ball representation in source Equation~(2.39), Wolf's
Proposition~2.10 writes the Pauli transfer matrix of a
Hermiticity-preserving map as
\begin{align}
    \widehat T
        &=
        \begin{pmatrix}
            \widehat T_{00}&r^{\mathsf T}\\
            v&\Delta
        \end{pmatrix}.
    \label{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_pauli_transfer_matrix}
\end{align}
It then asserts that the block truncation
\begin{align}
    \widehat T'
        &=\widehat T_{00}\oplus\Delta
    \label{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_pauli_block_truncation}
\end{align}
is positive, respectively completely positive, if and only if $\widehat T$ is
\cite[Proposition~2.10]{Wolf2012Quantum}. The local source is
\path{Notes/WolfNoteTexSource/ch02_representations.tex}, lines~984--990.

\paragraph{Correction.}
Define the reduction map
\begin{align}
    \Theta(X)
        &=\tr(X)\Id-X
    \label{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_pauli_reduction_map}
\end{align}
and the diagonal Pauli-coordinate matrix
\begin{align}
    D&=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1,-1,-1).
    \label{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_pauli_sign_matrix}
\end{align}
Then the truncation satisfies
\begin{align}
    T'
        &=\frac{T+\Theta\circ T\circ\Theta}{2},
    \label{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_pauli_map_average}\\
    \widehat T'
        &=\frac{\widehat T+D\widehat T D}{2},
    \label{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_pauli_matrix_average}\\
    \widehat T'
        &=\widehat T_{00}\oplus\Delta.
    \label{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_pauli_average_block_form}
\end{align}
The valid implications are
\begin{align}
    T\text{ positive}
        &\Longrightarrow T'\text{ positive},
    \label{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_pauli_positive_forward}\\
    T\text{ completely positive}
        &\Longrightarrow T'\text{ completely positive}.
    \label{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_pauli_cp_forward}
\end{align}
These are the directions proved at local source lines~992--998
\cite[Proposition~2.10]{Wolf2012Quantum} and formalized by the declarations
above.

\paragraph{Necessity of the correction.}
Consider
\begin{align}
    T(X)
        &=\tr(X)\operatorname{diag}(3/2,-1/2).
    \label{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_pauli_counterexample_map}
\end{align}
This map preserves Hermiticity but is not positive because
\begin{align}
    T(\Id/2)
        &=\operatorname{diag}(3/2,-1/2)
        \not\geq0.
    \label{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_pauli_counterexample_not_positive}
\end{align}
Its Pauli-block truncation is
\begin{align}
    T'(X)
        &=\tr(X)\frac{\Id}{2},
    \label{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_pauli_counterexample_truncation}
\end{align}
which is the completely depolarizing channel and is completely positive.
Hence both printed converses fail.

\paragraph{Classification.}
This is a source error. The proof of Proposition~2.10 supports
Eqs.~\ref{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_pauli_positive_forward} and
\ref{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_pauli_cp_forward}, not the printed
equivalences \cite{Wolf2012Quantum}.

\section{The inverse coefficient in the proof of Theorem~2.3}
\label{sec:ordered-cp-inverse}

\paragraph{Formalization trigger.}
The formal proof defines Wolf's normalized auxiliary matrix by
\leanid{stinespringW} and proves injectivity of the assignment from a supplied
Stinespring matrix by \leanid{stinespringW_injective}, both in
\doclink{QICLean/Channel/OrderedCP}.

\paragraph{Printed assertion.}
In the proof of Theorem~2.3, Wolf uses the normalized vector
\begin{align}
    \ket{\Omega}
        &=d'^{-1/2}\sum_a\ket{a,a}
    \label{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_ordered_cp_omega}
\end{align}
and defines
\begin{align}
    W_i
        &=(\one_{r_i}\otimes\bra{\Omega})
          (V_i\otimes\one_{d'}).
    \label{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_ordered_cp_w_definition}
\end{align}
The accompanying footnote states that $V_i\mapsto W_i$ is injective but prints
$d'^2$ as the coefficient in the inverse
\cite[Theorem~2.3]{Wolf2012Quantum}. The same coefficient occurs in
\path{Notes/WolfNoteTexSource/ch02_representations.tex}, lines~392--393, and
in the PDF on book p.~39.

\paragraph{Correction.}
The coordinate relation is
\begin{align}
    (W_i)_{j,(k,a)}
        &=d'^{-1/2}(V_i)_{(a,j),k}.
    \label{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_ordered_cp_coordinate_relation}
\end{align}
Its coordinate inverse therefore multiplies by $\sqrt{d'}$. With the
canonical tensor-factor identifications used in the source, the inverse is
\begin{align}
    V_i
        &=d'(W_i\otimes\one_{d'})
          (\one_d\otimes\ket{\Omega}).
    \label{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_ordered_cp_correct_inverse}
\end{align}

\paragraph{Necessity of the correction.}
The second occurrence of $\ket{\Omega}$ contributes another factor
$d'^{-1/2}$. Consequently,
\begin{align}
    (W_i\otimes\one_{d'})
    (\one_d\otimes\ket{\Omega})
        &=\frac{1}{d'}V_i.
    \label{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_ordered_cp_unscaled_inverse}
\end{align}
The coefficient in
Eq.~\ref{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_ordered_cp_correct_inverse} must
therefore be $d'$, not $d'^2$. Theorem~2.3 is unaffected: the formal
injectivity proof uses
Eq.~\ref{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_ordered_cp_coordinate_relation} and
cancels only the nonzero factor $d'^{-1/2}$.

\paragraph{Classification.}
This is a normalization typo in a source footnote. It changes neither the
statement nor the proof route of Theorem~2.3 \cite{Wolf2012Quantum}.

\section{The nonempty-family boundary in the Radon--Nikodym theorem}
\label{sec:radon-nikodym}

\paragraph{Formalization trigger.}
The source-facing theorem
\leanid{IsKrausCP.radon_nikodym_of_stinespring} assumes that the finite index
type is nonempty. It uses Wolf's rectangular Heisenberg dimensions
$T_i,T:\mathcal M_{d'}\to\mathcal M_d$ and
$V:\mathbb C^d\to\mathbb C^{d'}\otimes\mathbb C^r$. The boundary is
documented in
\path{docs/paper-gaps/wolf_radon_nikodym_nonempty_family.tex}.

\paragraph{Printed assertion.}
Wolf's Theorem~2.4 is the instrument-valued corollary of the preceding
comparison theorem for ordered completely positive maps. It fixes a
Stinespring representation of the total map and represents each component by
a positive effect on the same dilation space. The local source is
\path{Notes/WolfNoteTexSource/ch02_representations.tex}, lines~399--407. It
states:
\begin{quote}
``Let $\{T_i\}$ be a set of completely positive linear maps such that
$\sum_iT_i=T$ \ldots\ Then there is a set of positive operators $P_i\in M_r$
which sum up to $I_r$ such that
$T_i(A)=V^\dagger(A\otimes P_i)V$.''
\end{quote}
This is Theorem~2.4 of Ref.~\cite{Wolf2012Quantum}.

\paragraph{Formal statement.}
Let $\iota$ be a nonempty finite index type. The formal theorem assumes
completely positive maps
$T_i,T:\mathcal M_{d'}\to\mathcal M_d$ satisfying
\begin{align}
    \sum_{i\in\iota}T_i
        &=T
    \label{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_radon_nikodym_map_sum}
\end{align}
and a supplied map
$V:\mathbb C^d\to\mathbb C^{d'}\otimes\mathbb C^r$ satisfying
\begin{align}
    T(A)
        &=V^\dagger(A\otimes I_r)V.
    \label{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_radon_nikodym_total_dilation}
\end{align}
It concludes that positive semidefinite operators
$P_i\in\mathcal M_r$ exist such that
\begin{align}
    \sum_{i\in\iota}P_i
        &=I_r,
    \label{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_radon_nikodym_effect_sum}\\
    T_i(A)
        &=V^\dagger(A\otimes P_i)V.
    \label{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_radon_nikodym_component_formula}
\end{align}
The declaration \leanid{IsCPMap.radon_nikodym_of_stinespring} remains the
square-algebra specialization.

\paragraph{Formal proof path.}
Wolf presents this result as a simple corollary of the ordered-map comparison
theorem and gives no separate proof \cite[Theorem~2.4]{Wolf2012Quantum}. The
formal proof combines Kraus families for the components into an
outcome-labelled Stinespring representation $\widehat V$ of $T$, then applies
the preceding theorem to $T\leq T$ and the representations $\widehat V$ and
$V$. It obtains a contraction $C$ satisfying
\begin{align}
    \widehat V
        &=(I_{d'}\otimes C)V,
    \label{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_radon_nikodym_contraction_factorization}\\
    C^\dagger C
        &\leq I_r.
    \label{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_radon_nikodym_contraction_bound}
\end{align}
This contraction need not be an isometry.

Let $C_i$ retain the rows of $C$ labelled by $i$, and define
\begin{align}
    E_i
        &=C_i^\dagger C_i.
    \label{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_radon_nikodym_preliminary_effect}
\end{align}
The operators $E_i$ are positive, represent $T_i$, and satisfy
\begin{align}
    \sum_{i\in\iota}E_i
        &=C^\dagger C.
    \label{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_radon_nikodym_preliminary_sum}
\end{align}
The residual operator
\begin{align}
    R
        &=I_r-C^\dagger C
    \label{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_radon_nikodym_residual_definition}
\end{align}
is positive and satisfies
\begin{align}
    V^\dagger(A\otimes R)V
        &=0.
    \label{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_radon_nikodym_residual_vanishes}
\end{align}
Choose $i_0\in\iota$ and set
\begin{align}
    P_{i_0}
        &=E_{i_0}+R,
    \label{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_radon_nikodym_distinguished_effect}\\
    P_i
        &=E_i\qquad(i\neq i_0).
    \label{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_radon_nikodym_other_effects}
\end{align}
This step gives
Eq.~\ref{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_radon_nikodym_effect_sum} for an
arbitrary nonminimal supplied dilation and is where nonemptiness is used. If
$d'=0$, the formal proof instead assigns $I_r$ to a chosen outcome and zero
to every other outcome. The square-algebra declaration specializes the
rectangular theorem directly.

\paragraph{Necessity of the boundary.}
If $\iota$ is empty, then
Eq.~\ref{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_radon_nikodym_map_sum} forces $T=0$.
The zero Stinespring matrix $V=0$ satisfies
Eq.~\ref{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_radon_nikodym_total_dilation}, but the
printed conclusion would require
\begin{align}
    0
        =\sum_{i\in\iota}P_i
        &=I_r.
    \label{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_radon_nikodym_empty_contradiction}
\end{align}
This is false when the dilation space has nonzero dimension. A nonempty finite
outcome family is also the substantive case for a quantum instrument.

\paragraph{Classification.}
This is a local well-posedness correction, not a claim that the
nonempty-family Radon--Nikodym theorem is false. The word ``set'' and the
summation notation in the source leave the empty-family boundary implicit
\cite{Wolf2012Quantum}; Lean must state it explicitly.

\section{Suppressed zero multiplicities in the two-pure-state spectrum}
\label{sec:two-pure-state-spectrum}

\paragraph{Formalization trigger.}
The theorem
\leanid{Projectivization.charpoly_add_pureStateMatrix_of_two_le} retains the
full characteristic polynomial of the sum of two normalized pure-state
matrices. The correction is documented in
\path{docs/paper-gaps/wolf_ch01_two_pure_state_zero_multiplicities.tex}.

\paragraph{Printed assertion.}
In the spectrum-preserver argument following Wigner's theorem, Wolf states
that the two potentially nonzero eigenvalues of the sum of two Hermitian
rank-one projections $P,Q$ are
\begin{align}
    1\pm\sqrt{\tr(PQ)}.
    \label{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_two_pure_state_printed_values}
\end{align}
These values are the roots of
\begin{align}
    (X-1)^2-\tr(PQ).
    \label{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_two_pure_state_quadratic}
\end{align}
The statement appears in Chapter~1 of Ref.~\cite{Wolf2012Quantum}.

\paragraph{Correction.}
For $d\geq2$, the multiplicity-aware identity is
\begin{align}
    \chi_{P+Q}(X)
        &=X^{d-2}((X-1)^2-\tr(PQ)).
    \label{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_two_pure_state_charpoly}
\end{align}
The factor $X^{d-2}$ supplies the guaranteed additional zero multiplicity. If
$P=Q$, then
\begin{align}
    (X-1)^2-\tr(PQ)
        &=X(X-2),
    \label{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_two_pure_state_equal_projection_quadratic}
\end{align}
so the total zero multiplicity is $d-1$. In dimension one,
\begin{align}
    \chi_{P+Q}(X)
        &=X-2.
    \label{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_two_pure_state_dimension_one}
\end{align}
In dimension zero, no projective pure states exist.

\paragraph{Classification.}
This is a local correction to the spectrum shorthand in
Eq.~\ref{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_two_pure_state_printed_values}, not a
claim that the intended spectrum-preserver argument is false. The two
displayed values retain the transition-probability information used in that
argument \cite{Wolf2012Quantum}; the characteristic polynomial in
Eq.~\ref{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_two_pure_state_charpoly} records all
multiplicities.

\section{The rotation sign in the spinor exponential formula}
\label{sec:spinor-rotation-sign}

\paragraph{Formalization trigger.}
The rotation exponential is formalized by
\leanid{Wolf.spinorMatrix\_rotationExpSL2} in
\doclink{QICLean/Channel/LorentzNormalForm/SpinorExponential}. The discrepancy
is documented in
\path{docs/paper-gaps/wolf_ch2_spinor_rotation_sign.tex}.

\paragraph{Printed assertion.}
Source Equation~(2.44), at
\path{Notes/WolfNoteTexSource/ch02_representations.tex}, lines~1070--1077,
defines
\begin{align}
    R_i
        &=\sum_{j,k=1}^{3}\varepsilon_{ijk}\ketbra{k}{j}
    \label{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_spinor_rotation_generator}
\end{align}
and prints the correspondence
\begin{align}
    e^{-i\vec n\cdot\vec\sigma/2}
        &\longmapsto e^{-\vec n\cdot\vec R}.
    \label{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_spinor_printed_correspondence}
\end{align}
This is the rotation part of Equation~(2.44) in
Ref.~\cite{Wolf2012Quantum}.

\paragraph{Correction.}
With the standard Pauli matrices and the column-vector convention used in the
notes, the correct correspondence is
\begin{align}
    e^{-i\vec n\cdot\vec\sigma/2}
        &\longmapsto e^{+\vec n\cdot\vec R}.
    \label{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_spinor_corrected_correspondence}
\end{align}
Equivalently, the printed Lorentz exponential corresponds to a spinor with the
opposite sign in its exponent.

\paragraph{Necessity of the correction.}
For $\vec n=(0,0,\theta)$, direct Pauli conjugation gives
\begin{align}
    e^{-i\theta\sigma_3/2}\sigma_1e^{i\theta\sigma_3/2}
        &=\cos\theta\,\sigma_1+\sin\theta\,\sigma_2.
    \label{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_spinor_pauli_conjugation}
\end{align}
The generator in
Eq.~\ref{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_spinor_rotation_generator} satisfies
\begin{align}
    R_3\ket{1}
        &=\ket{2}.
    \label{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_spinor_generator_action}
\end{align}
Therefore
Eq.~\ref{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_spinor_pauli_conjugation} is the action
of $e^{+\theta R_3}$, whereas $e^{-\theta R_3}$ rotates toward the negative
second coordinate.

\paragraph{Classification.}
This is a source sign error. The formal theorem follows the actual congruence
action. The boost formula in the same source equation is unchanged
\cite[Equation~(2.44)]{Wolf2012Quantum}.

\section{The two boundary errors in Theorem~6.3}
\label{sec:theorem-6-3}

\paragraph{Formalization trigger.}
The declarations
\leanid{exists_wolfTheorem63_of_irreducible_positive} and
\leanid{exists_wolfTheorem63_of_irreducible_positive_of_ne_zero} formalize
Wolf's Theorem~6.3 for arbitrary positive maps. The declarations
\leanid{lowerCollatzWielandtValue},
\leanid{upperCollatzWielandtValue}, and

formalize the corrected pointwise and global quantities. Both corrections are

\paragraph{The global upper quantity.}
After defining the pointwise lower and upper functionals $r(X)$ and
$\widetilde r(X)$, local source lines~617--619 print
\begin{align}
    r
        &=\sup_{X\geq0}r(X),
    \label{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_theorem63_printed_lower_global}\\
    \widetilde r
        &=\sup_{X\geq0}\widetilde r(X),
    \label{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_theorem63_printed_upper_global}\\
    r
        &\geq\widetilde r.
    \label{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_theorem63_printed_global_order}
\end{align}
These formulas occur in the proof of Theorem~6.3 of
Ref.~\cite{Wolf2012Quantum}. The corrected Collatz--Wielandt quantities are
\begin{align}
    r
        &=\sup_{\substack{X\geq0\\X\neq0}}r(X),
    \label{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_theorem63_correct_lower_global}\\
    \widetilde r
        &=\inf_{\substack{X\geq0\\X\neq0}}\widetilde r(X).
    \label{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_theorem63_correct_upper_global}
\end{align}
For an irreducible positive map, both extrema are attained at the same
positive-definite eigenvector and are equal. Thus the second supremum and the
reversed inequality in
Eq.~\ref{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_theorem63_printed_global_order} are
source errors. Equality is not a pointwise statement for arbitrary $X$. In the
extended-real formulation,
\begin{align}
    r(0)&=+\infty,
    \label{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_theorem63_lower_at_zero}\\
    \widetilde r(0)&=-\infty,
    \label{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_theorem63_upper_at_zero}
\end{align}
whereas every nonzero $X\geq0$ satisfies
\begin{align}
    r(X)
        &\leq\widetilde r(X).
    \label{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_theorem63_pointwise_order}
\end{align}
Equality holds at positive eigenvectors, as used at local source
lines~649--651 \cite{Wolf2012Quantum}.

\paragraph{The one-dimensional zero map.}
Theorem~6.3 assumes only that $T:\MN{D}\to\MN{D}$ is positive and irreducible,
but its second item and proof require a strictly positive Perron value
\cite[Theorem~6.3]{Wolf2012Quantum}. On $\MN{1}$, the zero map is positive and
irreducible because $0$ and $\Id$ are the only orthogonal projections. Its
spectral radius is
\begin{align}
    r(T)
        &=0.
    \label{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_theorem63_zero_map_radius}
\end{align}
The theorem without a nonzero-map hypothesis must therefore allow $r=0$ while
retaining a positive-definite eigenvector $X>0$. The printed conclusion $r>0$
becomes valid after adding $T\neq0$. When $D>1$, irreducibility itself excludes
the zero map.

\paragraph{Classification.}
These are two local source errors exposed by the positive-map formalization.
They do not alter the proof for a nonzero irreducible map once the second
global extremum is interpreted as the infimum in
Eq.~\ref{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_theorem63_correct_upper_global}.

\section{The missing order hypothesis in the unitary comparison}
\label{sec:unitary-comparison}

\paragraph{Formalization trigger.}
The correction and the source proof route are documented in
\path{docs/paper-gaps/wolf_ch5_unitary_comparison_missing_order.tex}. The
cross-matrix Weyl comparison is formalized by
\leanid{LinearMap.IsSymmetric.eigenvalues\_le\_of\_sub\_isPositive}, and the
corrected proposition is formalized by
\leanid{Matrix.IsHermitian.exists\_unitary\_cfc\_le\_cfc\_of\_le}.

\paragraph{Printed assertion and correction.}
Immediately after stating Weyl monotonicity for Hermitian matrices satisfying
$A\leq B$, Proposition~5.3 omits this order hypothesis
\cite[Proposition~5.3]{Wolf2012Quantum}. The corrected statement, with the
unitary chosen before the interval and the function, is
\begin{align}
    A\leq B
        \Longrightarrow
        \exists U\in M_d(\mathbb C)\ \Bigl[
        &U^\dagger U=UU^\dagger=\Id
    \label{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_unitary_comparison_unitary}\\
        &{}\land
        \forall I\subseteq\mathbb R\ \forall f:I\to\mathbb R,\nonumber\\
        &\bigl(      I\text{ is an interval}
          \land
          \operatorname{spec}(A)\cup\operatorname{spec}(B)\subseteq I
          \land
          f\text{ is non-decreasing on }I
          \bigr)\nonumber\\
        &\hspace{4em}\Longrightarrow
          f(A)\leq Uf(B)U^\dagger
        \Bigr].
    \label{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_unitary_comparison_corrected_statement}
\end{align}
This is the corrected form of source Equation~(5.57)
\cite[Equation~(5.57)]{Wolf2012Quantum}.

In dimension one, take
\begin{align}
    A&=[1],
    \label{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_unitary_comparison_counterexample_a}\\
    B&=[0],
    \label{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_unitary_comparison_counterexample_b}\\
    I&=[0,1],
    \label{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_unitary_comparison_counterexample_interval}\\
    f(x)&=x.
    \label{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_unitary_comparison_counterexample_function}
\end{align}
Every unitary is a phase, so the unqualified printed conclusion becomes
\begin{align}
    1&\leq0,
    \label{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_unitary_comparison_counterexample_contradiction}
\end{align}
which is false. The hypothesis $A\leq B$ is indispensable.

\paragraph{Source proof route.}
Under $A\leq B$, source Equation~(5.56) gives
\begin{align}
    \lambda_j^\downarrow(A)
        &\leq\lambda_j^\downarrow(B)
    \label{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_unitary_comparison_eigenvalue_order}
\end{align}
for every $j$, because the smallest eigenvalue of $B-A$ is nonnegative
\cite[Equation~(5.56)]{Wolf2012Quantum}. Monotonicity of $f$ gives
\begin{align}
    f(\lambda_j^\downarrow(A))
        &\leq f(\lambda_j^\downarrow(B)).
    \label{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_unitary_comparison_function_order}
\end{align}
Choose unitaries $V_A,V_B$ whose columns are eigenvectors in decreasing
eigenvalue order, and define
\begin{align}
    U
        &=V_AV_B^\dagger.
    \label{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_unitary_comparison_basis_unitary}
\end{align}
Transporting
Eq.~\ref{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_unitary_comparison_function_order}
through these eigenbases yields
\begin{align}
    f(A)
        &\leq Uf(B)U^\dagger.
    \label{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_unitary_comparison_transported_order}
\end{align}
The unitary in
Eq.~\ref{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_unitary_comparison_basis_unitary}
depends only on the two ordered eigenbases, so it works for every admissible
$f$, as required by the quantifier order in Proposition~5.3
\cite{Wolf2012Quantum}. The formal cross-matrix eigenvalue comparison uses the
intersection of the span of the first $j+1$ ordered eigenvectors of $A$ with
the span of the last $d-j$ ordered eigenvectors of $B$; it does not assume a
pointwise comparison.

\paragraph{Classification.}
This is a source error. The printed theorem is false, whereas the corrected
statement in
Eq.~\ref{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_unitary_comparison_corrected_statement}
follows from the preceding source argument once $A\leq B$ is retained.

\section{The subunital logarithm clause in Corollary~5.2}
\label{sec:cor52-log}

\paragraph{Formalization trigger.}
The logarithmic Jensen declarations
\leanid{posMap_log_concave_jensen} and
\leanid{IsPositiveMap.log_concave_jensen}, in
\doclink{QICLean/Analysis/LiebConcavity} and
\doclink{QICLean/Channel/Schwarz/OperatorConvexity}, assume that the positive
map is unital. The discrepancy is documented in
\path{docs/paper-gaps/wolf_ch5_operator_jensen_lieb.tex}.

\paragraph{Printed assertion.}
Chapter~5, Corollary~5.2 places three conclusions under the common hypothesis
that $T$ is positive and $T(\one)\leq\one$. Its third conclusion is
\begin{align}
    T(\log A)
        &\leq\log(T(A)),
        \qquad A>0.
    \label{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_log_printed_subunital_clause}
\end{align}
The local source is
\path{Notes/WolfNoteTexSource/ch05_schwarz_inequalities.tex},
lines~761--769; see Corollary~5.2 of Ref.~\cite{Wolf2012Quantum}.

\paragraph{Correction.}
The proved logarithmic statement requires unitality:
\begin{align}
    T(\one)=\one,\qquad A>0
        &\Longrightarrow
        T(\log A)\leq\log(T(A)).
    \label{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_log_corrected_unital_clause}
\end{align}
The two real-power clauses in the same corollary remain valid under the
subunital hypothesis printed by Wolf \cite[Corollary~5.2]{Wolf2012Quantum}.
The live Jensen declarations use that hypothesis for the power clauses, so
the logarithmic correction does not alter them.

\paragraph{Necessity of the correction.}
Fix $0<c<1$ and consider the one-dimensional positive map
\begin{align}
    T_c(x)
        &=cx.
    \label{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_log_scalar_map}
\end{align}
For $A=1$,
\begin{align}
    T_c(1)
        &=c\leq1,
    \label{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_log_scalar_subunital}\\
    T_c(\log1)
        &=0,
    \label{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_log_scalar_left_side}\\
    \log(T_c(1))
        &=\log c<0.
    \label{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_log_scalar_right_side}
\end{align}
Hence
\begin{align}
    T_c(\log1)
        &>\log(T_c(1)),
    \label{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_log_scalar_contradiction}
\end{align}
so the printed clause fails even for a strictly positive scalar map. Wolf's
preceding positive-map monotonicity theorem also requires a function on an
interval containing zero with $f(0)\geq0$, whereas the logarithm is unbounded
below at zero \cite{Wolf2012Quantum}.

\paragraph{Classification.}
This is a source error in the common hypothesis of Corollary~5.2. The formal
theorem proves
Eq.~\ref{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_log_corrected_unital_clause} rather than
the false subunital target.

\section{Corrected errors in the local LaTeX transcription}
\label{app:transcription}

This appendix records errors found by comparing the source PDFs with the local

\subsection{The Choi marginal: $\tau_B$ versus $\tr_B\tau$}

\paragraph{Source context.}
Immediately after deriving the coordinate form of the Choi--Jamiolkowski
correspondence in source Equation~(2.4), Wolf describes the channel--state
dictionary \cite[Equation~(2.4)]{Wolf2012Quantum}. For a channel
$T:M_d\to M_{d'}$, the first tensor factor is the output factor $A$, and the
second is the untouched input or reference factor $B$. The PDF on book p.~34
states:
\begin{quote}
``If $T$ is a quantum channel in the Schr\"odinger picture, then $\tau$ is a
density operator with reduced density matrix $\tau_B=\one/d$.''
\end{quote}
Thus $\tau_B$ denotes $\tr_A\tau$.

\paragraph{Transcription and correction.}
A previous local transcription at
\path{ch02_representations.tex:113--114} replaced this marginal by
$\tr_B\tau=\one/d$. The source-faithful identity is
\begin{align}
    \tau_B
        =\tr_A\tau
        &=\frac{\one_d}{d}.
    \label{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_transcription_choi_correct_marginal}
\end{align}

\paragraph{Reason.}
Trace preservation is equivalent to
Eq.~\ref{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_transcription_choi_correct_marginal}.
The other marginal is
\begin{align}
    \tr_B\tau
        &=\frac{T(\one)}{d}.
    \label{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_transcription_choi_other_marginal}
\end{align}
It is maximally mixed precisely when $T$ is unital. The former transcription
therefore changed a trace-preserving condition into a stronger condition that
is generally false.

\subsection{The conjugation in the proof of Kramer's theorem}

\paragraph{Source context.}
Chapter~3 proves Kramer's theorem for a Hermitian matrix $H$ commuting with an
anti-unitary symmetry $T=\Gamma V$, where $V^T=-V$
\cite{Wolf2012Quantum}. After showing that
$V^\dagger\ket{\overline\psi}$ is another eigenvector, source
Equation~(3.29) proves orthogonality through
\begin{align}
    \bra{\psi}V^\dagger\ket{\overline\psi}
        &=-\bra{\psi}\overline V\ket{\overline\psi},
    \label{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_transcription_kramers_first_equality}\\
    \bra{\psi}\overline V\ket{\overline\psi}
        &=\overline{\bra{\overline\psi}V\ket{\psi}}.
    \label{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_transcription_kramers_conjugation}
\end{align}
This is Equation~(3.29) in Ref.~\cite{Wolf2012Quantum}. The final conjugate is
then identified with
$\bra{\psi}V^\dagger\ket{\overline\psi}$, so the scalar equals its own
negative.

\paragraph{Transcription and correction.}
A previous local TeX version at
\path{ch03_positive_not_completely.tex:515} omitted the outer conjugation in
the last term. The corrected term is
\begin{align}
    -\overline{\bra{\overline\psi}V\ket{\psi}}.
    \label{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_transcription_kramers_correct_term}
\end{align}

\paragraph{Reason.}
The componentwise identity in
Eq.~\ref{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_transcription_kramers_conjugation}
shows that the conjugation is necessary. Without it, the equality is false in
general, and the final self-cancellation does not follow.

\subsection{The order of the conjugated factors in Example~5.3}

\paragraph{Source context.}
Chapter~5 considers the adjoint of the map
\begin{align}
    T(X)
        &=\frac12\left(X^T+\frac12\tr(X)\one\right)
    \label{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_transcription_example53_map}
\end{align}
as an example of a Schwarz map that need not be $2$-positive
\cite[Example~5.3]{Wolf2012Quantum}. For traceless $A$, source
Equation~(5.10) subtracts
\begin{align}
    \frac14\,\overline A A^T
    \label{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_transcription_example53_source_product}
\end{align}
from $T^*(A^\dagger A)$
\cite[Equation~(5.10)]{Wolf2012Quantum}.

\paragraph{Transcription and correction.}
A previous local TeX version at
\path{ch05_schwarz_inequalities.tex:373,377} wrote
$A\overline A^{\,T}$. It has been corrected to
Eq.~\ref{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_transcription_example53_source_product}.

\paragraph{Reason.}
For traceless $A$,
\begin{align}
    T^*(A)
        &=\frac12A^T,
    \label{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_transcription_example53_adjoint}\\
    T^*(A^\dagger)T^*(A)
        &=\frac14(A^\dagger)^T A^T,
    \label{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_transcription_example53_product_first}\\
    T^*(A^\dagger)T^*(A)
        &=\frac14\,\overline A A^T.
    \label{eq:wolf_lecture_notes_errata_transcription_example53_product_correct}
\end{align}
The transcribed matrix
$A\overline A^{\,T}=AA^\dagger$ is generally different. Both matrices are
positive semidefinite, so the final positivity conclusion in the example
survives.
