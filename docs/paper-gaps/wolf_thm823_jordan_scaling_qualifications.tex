\section{The source statement}

For a linear map $T$, Wolf defines $\mu$ as the largest modulus of an
eigenvalue of $T$ in the open unit disc. He defines $d_\mu$ as the largest
dimension of a Jordan block associated with an eigenvalue of modulus $\mu$
\cite[Chapter~8, lines~1221--1272]{Wolf2012Quantum}. The theorem
``Asymptotic convergence I'' compares the difference between the powers of the
phase-weighted map $\widehat T$ and its peripheral part
$\widehat T_\varphi$ with the scale
\begin{align}
    \mu^n n^{d_\mu-1}.
    \label{eq:wolf_thm823_jordan_scaling_qualifications_asymptotic_scale}
\end{align}
More precisely, the source compares
\begin{align}
    \left\lVert \widehat T^n-\widehat T_\varphi^n\right\rVert_\infty
        &\quad\text{with}\quad
        \mu^n n^{d_\mu-1}.
    \label{eq:wolf_thm823_jordan_scaling_qualifications_source_comparison}
\end{align}
The local source is
\path{Notes/WolfNoteTexSource/ch08_distance_measures.tex},
lines~1221--1272, especially Equations~(8.106)--(8.109) of
Ref.~\cite{Wolf2012Quantum}.

\section{Empty and zero subperipheral spectra}

If $T$ has no eigenvalue in the open unit disc, neither the largest modulus
$\mu$ nor the associated block size $d_\mu$ is defined in the printed
statement of Ref.~\cite{Wolf2012Quantum}. The formal definition totalizes only
the modulus by setting
\begin{align}
    \mu
        &= 0.
    \label{eq:wolf_thm823_jordan_scaling_qualifications_empty_modulus}
\end{align}
On a nonempty subperipheral spectrum, finiteness shows that the defining
supremum is an attained maximum strictly below one.\footnote{Formal
declarations:
\leanid{Module.End.subperipheralModulus},
\leanid{Module.End.exists_norm_eq_subperipheralModulus}, and
\leanid{Module.End.subperipheralModulus_lt_one} in
\doclink{QICLean/Analysis/SubperipheralSpectrum.lean}.}

The equality in
(\ref{eq:wolf_thm823_jordan_scaling_qualifications_empty_modulus}) does not
imply that the subperipheral spectrum is empty. It means that every
subperipheral eigenvalue is zero. A zero-eigenvalue Jordan block of dimension
$D>1$ is nilpotent, but its powers need not vanish when $1\leq n<D$.
Consequently, an upper bound of the form
\begin{align}
    C_2\mu^n n^{d_\mu-1}
        &= 0
    \label{eq:wolf_thm823_jordan_scaling_qualifications_zero_modulus_bound}
\end{align}
cannot control those initial powers.

A classical three-state transition gives a concrete example. Let state~$1$
map to itself, state~$2$ map to state~$1$, and state~$3$ map to state~$2$.
The corresponding column-stochastic matrix is
\begin{align}
    P
        &=
        \begin{pmatrix}
            1 & 1 & 0\\
            0 & 0 & 1\\
            0 & 0 & 0
        \end{pmatrix}.
    \label{eq:wolf_thm823_jordan_scaling_qualifications_transition_matrix}
\end{align}
For the standard basis vectors $e_1,e_2,e_3$, one has
\begin{align}
    P(e_3-e_1)
        &= e_2-e_1 \neq 0,
    \label{eq:wolf_thm823_jordan_scaling_qualifications_jordan_chain_first}\\
    P(e_2-e_1)
        &= 0.
    \label{eq:wolf_thm823_jordan_scaling_qualifications_jordan_chain_second}
\end{align}
Equations
(\ref{eq:wolf_thm823_jordan_scaling_qualifications_jordan_chain_first}) and
(\ref{eq:wolf_thm823_jordan_scaling_qualifications_jordan_chain_second})
exhibit a size-two Jordan chain at zero. The associated measure-and-prepare
quantum channel is positive and trace preserving. Its phase-weighted
peripheral map annihilates this chain. At $n=1$, the left-hand side of
Equation~(8.107) of Ref.~\cite{Wolf2012Quantum} is therefore nonzero, whereas
the printed right-hand side is zero.

The zero-modulus case is thus not merely a boundary case excluded by the
source hypotheses. A correct zero-modulus statement must either begin at the
nilpotence index or treat the finite initial range separately without the
factor in
(\ref{eq:wolf_thm823_jordan_scaling_qualifications_zero_modulus_bound}).
The source uses $n\in\mathbb N$ as a positive exponent in the proof at
lines~1247--1266 of Ref.~\cite{Wolf2012Quantum}. In Lean, natural numbers
include zero, so the zeroth power additionally requires an explicit convention
for $0^0$ and does not belong to that source argument.

For $0<\mu\leq 1$ and $d_\mu-1\leq n$, the exact fixed-block consequence of
Equation~(8.104) of Ref.~\cite{Wolf2012Quantum} is formalized as a two-sided
estimate for one supplied Jordan block.\footnote{Formal declaration:
\leanid{Matrix.l2_opNorm_jordanBlock_pow_polynomial_bounds} in
\doclink{QICLean/Analysis/JordanBlockAsymptotics.lean}.}
This result proves the scale in
(\ref{eq:wolf_thm823_jordan_scaling_qualifications_asymptotic_scale}) for a
single block. It does not extend that formula across the zero-modulus
boundary.

\section{The block-diagonal comparison}

Let $J$ be a block-diagonal Jordan matrix, and let $B$ be one of its blocks.
At source line~1261, the notes assert
\begin{align}
    \lVert J^n\rVert_\infty
        &\leq \lVert B^n\rVert_\infty.
    \label{eq:wolf_thm823_jordan_scaling_qualifications_printed_block_bound}
\end{align}
This is the inequality printed for the block denoted by $X$ in
Ref.~\cite{Wolf2012Quantum}. In the Hilbert-space operator norm, an arbitrary
chosen block instead satisfies
\begin{align}
    \lVert B^n\rVert_\infty
        &\leq \lVert J^n\rVert_\infty.
    \label{eq:wolf_thm823_jordan_scaling_qualifications_valid_block_bound}
\end{align}
The intended eventual-dominance argument must select a block with largest
subperipheral modulus and, among blocks tied in modulus, largest dimension. It
must then prove that this block realizes the maximum of all block norms for
sufficiently large $n$. Only that argument yields the eventual equality
\begin{align}
    \lVert J^n\rVert_\infty
        &= \lVert B^n\rVert_\infty.
    \label{eq:wolf_thm823_jordan_scaling_qualifications_eventual_block_equality}
\end{align}

\section{The Jordan condition infimum}

Let $L$ be a matrix similar to a Jordan matrix $J$, and choose an invertible
matrix $A$ such that
\begin{align}
    L
        &= AJA^{-1}.
    \label{eq:wolf_thm823_jordan_scaling_qualifications_similarity}
\end{align}
Submultiplicativity gives the basis-level comparison
\begin{align}
    \bigl(\lVert A\rVert_\infty
          \lVert A^{-1}\rVert_\infty\bigr)^{-1}
    \lVert J^n\rVert_\infty
        &\leq \lVert L^n\rVert_\infty,
    \label{eq:wolf_thm823_jordan_scaling_qualifications_similarity_lower}\\
    \lVert L^n\rVert_\infty
        &\leq
        \lVert A\rVert_\infty
        \lVert A^{-1}\rVert_\infty
        \lVert J^n\rVert_\infty.
    \label{eq:wolf_thm823_jordan_scaling_qualifications_similarity_upper}
\end{align}
This comparison is formalized for the chosen basis.\footnote{Formal
declaration:
\leanid{Matrix.l2_opNorm_pow_similarity_bounds} in
\doclink{QICLean/Analysis/JordanSimilarity.lean}.}

Equation~(8.108) of Ref.~\cite{Wolf2012Quantum} instead uses the infimum
$\kappa_T$ over all Jordan changes of basis. Replacing the chosen factor in
(\ref{eq:wolf_thm823_jordan_scaling_qualifications_similarity_lower}) and
(\ref{eq:wolf_thm823_jordan_scaling_qualifications_similarity_upper}) by this
infimum requires either attainment of the infimum or a limiting argument
compatible with the selected normalized Jordan form. The imported Lean Jordan
API does not currently provide such a result, so the formal declaration does
not assume a minimizing basis.

Under the detailed-balance hypothesis in Equation~(8.110) of
Ref.~\cite{Wolf2012Quantum}, the formal development constructs Wolf's chosen
diagonalizing change of basis and its inverse:
\begin{align}
    A
        &= S^{1/2}U,
    \label{eq:wolf_thm823_jordan_scaling_qualifications_detailed_balance_basis}\\
    A^{-1}
        &= U^\dagger S^{-1/2}.
    \label{eq:wolf_thm823_jordan_scaling_qualifications_detailed_balance_inverse}
\end{align}
It also obtains the condition factor
\begin{align}
    \lVert A\rVert_\infty\lVert A^{-1}\rVert_\infty
        &=
        \sqrt{\lVert S\rVert_\infty
              \lVert S^{-1}\rVert_\infty}.
    \label{eq:wolf_thm823_jordan_scaling_qualifications_detailed_balance_factor}
\end{align}
These constructions and the norm calculation are formalized
directly.\footnote{Formal declaration:
\leanid{Matrix.PosDef.exists_sqrt_mul_unitary_diagonalization_of_detailedBalance}
in \doclink{QICLean/Channel/DetailedBalance.lean}.}
The formal statement deliberately does not rename the chosen factor in
(\ref{eq:wolf_thm823_jordan_scaling_qualifications_detailed_balance_factor})
as $\kappa_T$.

Completing the printed $\kappa_T$ bound still requires the source-shaped
infimum tracked in
\href{https://github.com/LionSR/QICLean/issues/297}{QICLean issue \#297} and a
proof that this infimum is bounded above by the condition factor of every
admissible Jordan change of basis. The detailed-balance assembly is tracked in
\href{https://github.com/LionSR/QICLean/issues/298}{QICLean issue \#298}.

\section{Verdict}

\textbf{Verdict: source qualifications and missing Jordan-form
infrastructure.} The shared modulus, the basis-level similarity inequalities
in
(\ref{eq:wolf_thm823_jordan_scaling_qualifications_similarity_lower}) and
(\ref{eq:wolf_thm823_jordan_scaling_qualifications_similarity_upper}), and the
positive-modulus single-block scaling are proved. The full statement for
$\widehat T^n-\widehat T_\varphi^n$ still requires a constructive Jordan
decomposition, largest-block data $d_\mu$, the direct-sum norm maximum,
eventual dominance, the zero-modulus and positive-exponent qualifications, and
a justified passage to $\kappa_T$. Until these dependencies are supplied,
Equations~(8.106)--(8.109) of Ref.~\cite{Wolf2012Quantum} should not be marked
formalized. The remaining work is tracked in
\href{https://github.com/LionSR/QICLean/issues/297}{QICLean issue \#297}.
