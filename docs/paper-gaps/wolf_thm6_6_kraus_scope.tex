\section{Source contract}

Let $D\geq1$ and let $T:\MN{D}\to\MN{D}$ be a positive unital irreducible
complex-linear map satisfying the Schwarz inequality. Theorem~6.6 of
Ref.~\cite{Wolf2012Quantum}, at lines~756--769 of
\path{Notes/WolfNoteTexSource/ch06_spectral_properties.tex}, asserts four
conclusions. The peripheral spectrum is a cyclic group of order
$1\leq m\leq D^2$, all its eigenspaces are one-dimensional, a unitary $U$
satisfies $T(U^k)=\gamma^kU^k$ for all $k\in\mathbb{N}$, and its spectral
projections satisfy $T(P_{k+1})=P_k$, where $\gamma=\exp(2\pi i/m)$.
Neither complete positivity nor trace preservation is assumed.

\section{Elimination of the earlier restrictions}

The earlier cyclicity theorem assumed a finite Kraus representation and a
supplied positive-definite fixed point of the adjoint. Those remain valid
specializations, with their established theorem signatures retained. The
abstract theorem now proves all four clauses without either assumption or
trace preservation of $T$.\footnote{Formal declaration:
\leanid{SchwarzMap.wolf_theorem_6_6} in
\path{QICLean/Channel/Peripheral/IrreducibleSchwarz.lean}.}

Since $T(\Id)=\Id$, the abstract left Perron theorem at eigenvalue one gives
$\rho>0$ with $T^*(\rho)=\rho$. Division by its positive trace yields a
stationary density matrix. Thus the faithful invariant state is derived
from the source hypotheses. Invariance under the bilinear trace adjoint,
not trace preservation of $T$, is used to annihilate the positive Schwarz
defect at a peripheral eigenvector.

For a peripheral eigenvector $X$, equality in Schwarz gives
$T(X^\dagger X)=X^\dagger X$. The ordinary Perron eigenspace is spanned by
$\Id$, so $X^\dagger X=c\Id$ with $c>0$. Rescaling gives a unitary
peripheral eigenvector. The abstract multiplicative-domain identities imply
product closure of the peripheral spectrum and one-dimensionality of every
peripheral eigenspace.

Let $S$ denote that spectrum and set $m=|S|$. Multiplication by any
$\mu\in S$ permutes the finite set $S$. Comparing the products of all
its elements gives $\mu^m=1$. Cardinality comparison with the $m$-th roots
of unity proves $S=\{\gamma^k:0\leq k<m\}$. Choosing one nonzero eigenvector
for each distinct eigenvalue gives a linearly independent family, hence
$m\leq\dim_{\C}\MN{D}=D^2$.

\section{Phase normalization and cyclic projections}

The power identity gives $T(U^m)=U^m$, whence $U^m=\alpha\Id$ for some
$|\alpha|=1$. The source proof at lines~791--793 passes directly from a
scalar fixed point to $U^m=\Id$. The formal proof makes the needed phase
choice explicit: select $\beta$ with $\beta^m=\alpha^{-1}$ and replace $U$
by $\beta U$. Then $|\beta|=1$, the peripheral eigenvalue is unchanged,
and $U^m=\Id$.

The existing abstract Fourier projection construction gives
$P_k=m^{-1}\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\overline{\gamma}^{kj}U^j$. These are orthogonal
projections resolving $\Id$, with $U=\sum_k\gamma^kP_k$ and
$T(P_{k+1})=P_k$. Their pairwise orthogonality is also explicit in the
assembled statement.

\section{Verdict}

\textbf{Verdict: resolved scope restriction.} The source-general theorem
contains all four conclusions, the canonical phase, and the bound
$m\leq D^2$. The finite-Kraus cyclicity and peripheral-unitary declarations
now specialize the abstract development. The theorem states ordinary
one-dimensional eigenspaces, as requested; no assertion about algebraic
multiplicity is inferred merely from geometric simplicity.
