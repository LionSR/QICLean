\gapnote{local-correction}{resolved}

Hastings, \emph{Solving gapped Hamiltonians locally},
\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0508554v3}{arXiv:cond-mat/0508554v3},
source \path{proj3new.tex}, lines 1695--1708, sketches successive
spectral refinements of compressed observables. The construction below retains
both cross terms introduced by every compression. It provides a corrected
auxiliary estimate, rather than the printed quantitative constant.

\section{The compression identity}
For an orthogonal projection $P$, put $Q=I-P$. Then
\[
 [PBP,PCP]=P[B,C]P-PBQCP+PCQBP.
\]
If the original commutator is bounded by $Ck(i,l)$ and the two cross
directions are bounded by $D\sqrt{k(i,j)}$, the multiplicative triangle
inequality $k(i,j)k(j,l)\le k(i,l)$ gives
\[
 \|[PB_iP,PB_lP]\|\le(C+2D^2)\sqrt{k(i,l)},\qquad 0\le k\le1.
\]
The two cross terms cannot be dropped.

\section{An actual basis without inverse spectral gaps}
For a finite self-adjoint family on an $n$-dimensional complex Hilbert space,
choose a member of maximal spectral spread $\rho$. If $\rho=0$, every member
is scalar and any orthonormal basis is terminal, even when $n>1$.
Otherwise an adjacent spectral gap is at least $\rho/(n-1)$ and gives a
proper nonzero orthogonal projection. The elementary bound by $\rho$,
combined with the commutator bound divided by $\rho$, controls cross blocks
by $\sqrt{n^3C}\sqrt{k(i,j)}$. Both child dimensions are smaller.

With total dimension $N$, the coefficient $C_t=C(1+2N^3)^t$ controls depth $t$.
At most $N-1$ original members are selected, counted with multiplicity along
the two subtrees. Put $A_N(C)=\sqrt{N^3C(1+2N^3)^N}$,
$k_0=k$ and $k_{r+1}=\sqrt{k_r}$. The constructed orthonormal basis satisfies
\[
 |\langle v_a,B_iv_c\rangle|
 \le A_N(C)w_i\sum_{j\in\mathcal S}k_N(i,j),\qquad a\ne c,
\]
provided $\|B_i\|\le w_i$, $w_i\ge0$, and
$\|[B_i,B_j]\|\le Cw_iw_jk(i,j)$. Zero weights give zero operators.
For exponential distance decay the rate becomes $\gamma/2^N$.

This estimate is uniform over the family for fixed $N,C$ and kernel. It
assumes no bound on an inverse spectral spacing or inverse commutator-map
condition number. Physical summation and its geometric growth requirement
are separate; no tree counterexample theorem is asserted here.
