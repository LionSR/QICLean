\section{Source assertion}

The companion development of Ref.~\cite{OpenAI2026PEPSFiltersEnergy}
constructs a norm-maximizing product of nested filters on a square grid.
For a local dimension $q$, interaction bound $J$, normalized unique ground
vector $\Omega$ of energy $E_0$, and full-system gap $\Delta$, it permits
weights $0<a_j\le1$. Each edge crosses at most one filtering region $D_j$.
If $b_j$ counts the edges crossing $D_j$, the selected normalized output
$\phi$ satisfies
\begin{align}
  |\langle\phi,H\phi\rangle-E_0|
    &\le q^2J\sum_j b_j a_j^2,
    \label{eq:zero_floor_source_energy}\\
  \Delta(1-|\langle\Omega,\phi\rangle|^2)
    &\le q^2J\sum_j b_j a_j^2.
    \label{eq:zero_floor_source_overlap}
\end{align}
The source takes a compact limit of regularized maximizing families,
proves that the limiting maximal output norm is positive, and then passes
the energy and overlap bounds through normalization.\footnote{Source
\leanid{exists_maximizer_energy}, lines 62--109 of
\href{https://github.com/openai/math/blob/adc7f1241b42e322a6451854ab7e4b4c146bf78a/lean/OAI/MathematicalPhysics/PEPSFilters/EnergyOptimizer.lean}{EnergyOptimizer.lean}.
The positive-regularization estimate is
\leanid{exists_regularized_maximizer_energy}, lines 29--60.}

\section{The preserved coarse estimate}

The original statements apply to arbitrary finite sites and supported
Hermitian terms $H=\sum_i h_i$, with $\|h_i\|\le c_0$ and
$H\Omega=E_0\Omega$, where $\Omega\ne0$. They assume nested regions,
$0<a_j\le1/2$, and that each support $S_i$ is split by at most one region.
If $C_j$ is the set of terms split by $D_j$, their energy budget is
\begin{align}
  B_{\mathrm{coarse}}
    &=\sum_j4a_j^2\sum_{i\in C_j}(\dim\mathcal H_{S_i})^2c_0.
    \label{eq:zero_floor_coarse_constant}
\end{align}
One floor-zero norm maximizer has nonzero output and normalized energy
excess at most $B_{\mathrm{coarse}}$.\footnote{The unchanged declarations are
\leanid{Entropy.exists_max_feasible_chain_zero_floor_energy} and
\leanid{Entropy.exists_max_feasible_chain_zero_floor_normalized_energy}
in \doclink{QICLean/Entropy/FilterZeroFloorEnergy}.}
These statements retain their original hypotheses and conclusions.

\section{The sharp arbitrary-site estimate}

The sharp statements allow $0<a_j\le1$ under the same nesting,
single-split and eigenvector hypotheses. Define
\begin{align}
  d_{ij}&=\dim\mathcal H_{S_i\cap D_j},\notag\\
  B_{\mathrm{in}}&=\sum_j a_j^2\sum_{i\in C_j}d_{ij}^2c_0.
  \label{eq:zero_floor_inside_budget}
\end{align}
At a positive-floor norm maximum, every filter is clipped relative to
the regional state of the output. The sharp stationary-chain estimate
gives the one-sided energy bound with $B_{\mathrm{in}}$.
Apply it again to $-h_i$ and eigenvalue $-E_0$, using the same
maximizing family. This gives the absolute energy bound without any
ground-state hypothesis.\footnote{The positive-floor declarations are
\leanid{Entropy.re_inner_sub_le_of_max_sharp} and
\leanid{Entropy.abs_re_inner_sub_le_of_max_sharp}; they use
\leanid{Entropy.forall_clipped_of_max} and
\leanid{Entropy.re_inner_sub_le_of_chain_sharp}. See
\doclink{QICLean/Entropy/FilterChain} and
\doclink{QICLean/Entropy/FilterChainEnergy}.}

Pass the closed homogeneous condition to a limiting maximizing family:
\begin{align}
  |\operatorname{Re}\langle\psi,H\psi\rangle-E_0\|\psi\|^2|
    &\le B_{\mathrm{in}}\|\psi\|^2.
    \label{eq:zero_floor_sharp_homogeneous}
\end{align}
A fixed strictly positive uniform competitor proves $\psi\ne0$.
The same family's normalized output $\phi=\psi/\|\psi\|$ therefore has
unit norm and absolute energy error at most $B_{\mathrm{in}}$.
No gap, ground-state assumption or normalized input is needed for this
assertion. Empty filter chains and empty interaction families are
allowed; no separate sign assumption on $c_0$ is imposed.\footnote{The
sharp zero-floor declarations are
\leanid{Entropy.exists_max_feasible_chain_zero_floor_abs_energy_sharp}
and
\leanid{Entropy.exists_max_feasible_chain_zero_floor_normalized_abs_energy_sharp}.
They use the same closed-condition limit and nonvanishing results as
the coarse statements.}
For a normalized input and a positive full-system gap, the gap estimate
also yields overlap squared at least $1-B_{\mathrm{in}}/\Delta$ for
this same chosen output.

\section{Remaining source comparison}

For one-site and two-site nearest-neighbor interactions, no one-site term
crosses a cut, and each crossing two-site support has exactly one inside
endpoint. Thus $d_{ij}=q$ for each crossing term and
$B_{\mathrm{in}}=q^2J\sum_j b_j a_j^2$. The generic weight range and
dimension coefficient now agree with those of
(\ref{eq:zero_floor_source_energy}). The exact nearest-neighbor
counting identity is already established separately in TNLean; it is
not reproved here.\footnote{See
\leanid{TNLean.PEPS.AreaLaw.nearestNeighbor_crossing_budget} in
\href{https://github.com/LionSR/TNLean/blob/0afd1775b8ef7861082bab9a4d0fcb5f006792ac/TNLean/PEPS/AreaLaw/EdgeCrossingBudget.lean\#L154-L172}{EdgeCrossingBudget.lean}.}

The comparison with the complete companion-source assertion remains open.
It still requires identification of the source's positive filters
with trace-power one and the floor-zero feasible filters, identification
of the ordered lifted products and their maximizing properties, and
the physical grid specialization, including the Hermitian-term
reduction and original full-system gap. The new absolute estimate
does not require a ground-state lower-bound argument.

The geometric producer additionally needs its uniform collar and
harmonic-weight construction, the specified weight sum, and its upper
norm comparison. None of those geometric conclusions is asserted by the
generic energy estimates.\footnote{The selective-source comparison remains
tracked in \href{https://github.com/LionSR/TNLean/issues/8741}{TNLean issue 8741}.}
This note records the remaining difference of representations and
physical conclusions, rather than an error in the source assertion.
