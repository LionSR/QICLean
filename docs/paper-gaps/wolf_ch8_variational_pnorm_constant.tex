\section{The printed statement}
Wolf's Theorem ``Variational ways to $p$-norms'' \cite{Wolf2012Quantum},
local source \path{Notes/WolfNoteTexSource/ch08_distance_measures.tex},
lines~178--187, states for $P\ge0$, $p\ge1$ and $p^{-1}+q^{-1}=1$
\begin{equation}\tag{8.13}
    \|P\|_p^p=\frac1p\sup_{X\ge0}\Bigl(\operatorname{tr}[PX]-\frac1q\|X\|_q^q\Bigr).
\end{equation}

\section{The factor belongs to the left-hand side}
Take $d=1$ and $P=t\ge0$. For $x\ge0$ Young's inequality gives
$tx\le t^p/p+x^q/q$, with equality at $x=t^{p-1}$, so the supremum equals
$t^p/p$. The printed right-hand side is therefore $t^p/p^2$, which differs from
$t^p$ unless $t=0$ or $p=1$. The correct identity is
\begin{equation*}
    \frac1p\|P\|_p^p=\sup_{X\ge0}\Bigl(\operatorname{tr}[PX]-\frac1q\|X\|_q^q\Bigr),
\end{equation*}
equivalently $\|P\|_p^p=p\sup_{X\ge0}[\cdots]$. In general the upper bound is
Hölder's inequality (8.6) followed by Young's inequality, and $X=P^{p-1}$
attains it since $(p-1)q=p$.

The formula also needs $p>1$: for $p=1$ the conjugate exponent is $q=\infty$,
and $\frac1q\|X\|_q^q$ has no meaning. The formalization takes $p,q$ real Hölder
conjugates, which forces $1<p,q<\infty$.

\section{Formalization}
The corrected identity is proved for every positive semidefinite $P$ on a
finite index type, as an attained supremum, in
\doclink{QICLean/Analysis/SchattenVariational}. No other hypothesis is added.
