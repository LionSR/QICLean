\section{Source statement}

Let $\{T_i\}_{i\in I}$ be a finite family of completely positive linear maps,
and let $T$ be another completely positive map, with
\begin{align}
    T_i,T:\mathcal M_{d'}&\longrightarrow\mathcal M_d,
    \label{eq:wolf_radon_nikodym_nonempty_family_map_types}\\
    \sum_{i\in I}T_i&=T.
    \label{eq:wolf_radon_nikodym_nonempty_family_map_sum}
\end{align}
Suppose that $T$ has a supplied Stinespring representation
\begin{align}
    V:\C^d&\longrightarrow\C^{d'}\otimes\C^r,
    \label{eq:wolf_radon_nikodym_nonempty_family_stinespring_map}\\
    T(A)&=V^\dagger(A\otimes\Id_r)V.
    \label{eq:wolf_radon_nikodym_nonempty_family_stinespring_formula}
\end{align}
Wolf's Radon--Nikodym theorem for quantum instruments states that there are
positive operators $P_i\in\mathcal M_r$ satisfying
\begin{align}
    \sum_{i\in I}P_i&=\Id_r,
    \label{eq:wolf_radon_nikodym_nonempty_family_effect_resolution}\\
    T_i(A)&=V^\dagger(A\otimes P_i)V.
    \label{eq:wolf_radon_nikodym_nonempty_family_component_representation}
\end{align}
This statement appears in
\path{Notes/WolfNoteTexSource/ch02_representations.tex}, lines 399--407, under
the title ``Radon--Nikodym for quantum instruments'' in
Ref.~\cite{Wolf2012Quantum}. The two preceding lines describe it as a corollary
of the comparison theorem for ordered completely positive maps
\cite{Wolf2012Quantum}.

The source does not state separately that the outcome set $I$ is finite. The
formal theorem interprets the unqualified sum in
(\ref{eq:wolf_radon_nikodym_nonempty_family_map_sum}) as a finite sum, in
accordance with the surrounding finite-dimensional development.

\section{The nonempty-family boundary}

The source-facing formal theorem assumes that the finite index type $I$ is
nonempty.\footnote{Formal declaration:
    \leanid{IsKrausCP.radon_nikodym_of_stinespring}.}
This condition does not strengthen the substantive instrument theorem. It
records the boundary required for
(\ref{eq:wolf_radon_nikodym_nonempty_family_effect_resolution}) to be a
resolution of the identity by instrument components.

If $I$ is empty, then
(\ref{eq:wolf_radon_nikodym_nonempty_family_map_sum}) implies
\begin{align}
    T&=0.
    \label{eq:wolf_radon_nikodym_nonempty_family_empty_total_map}
\end{align}
The zero map admits the supplied Stinespring matrix $V=0$. However, the
conclusion would require
\begin{align}
    \sum_{i\in\varnothing}P_i
        &=0
    \label{eq:wolf_radon_nikodym_nonempty_family_empty_effect_sum}\\
        &=\Id_r.
    \label{eq:wolf_radon_nikodym_nonempty_family_empty_identity_requirement}
\end{align}
This is false when the dilation dimension $r$ is nonzero. If $r=0$, the two
sides agree, but nonemptiness is the uniform condition valid for every supplied
dilation.

The formal theorem therefore states the source result for nonempty finite
instruments and makes the rectangular Heisenberg dimensions explicit. The
older declaration \leanid{IsCPMap.radon_nikodym_of_stinespring} remains the
square-algebra specialization.

\section{The formal proof}

Choose rectangular Kraus families for the component maps and combine them into
an outcome-labelled Stinespring representation $\widehat V$ of $T$. Apply the
ordered-map comparison theorem to the reflexive relation $T\leq T$ and the two
representations $\widehat V$ and $V$. This gives a contraction $C$ such that
\begin{align}
    \widehat V
        &=(\Id_{d'}\otimes C)V,
    \label{eq:wolf_radon_nikodym_nonempty_family_comparison_factorization}\\
    C^\dagger C&\leq\Id_r.
    \label{eq:wolf_radon_nikodym_nonempty_family_contraction_bound}
\end{align}

Restrict the rows of $C$ to the outcome $i$ and call the resulting matrix
$C_i$. Define
\begin{align}
    E_i&=C_i^\dagger C_i.
    \label{eq:wolf_radon_nikodym_nonempty_family_preliminary_effect}
\end{align}
Each $E_i$ is positive and represents the corresponding component map. The
row decomposition gives
\begin{align}
    \sum_{i\in I}E_i
        &=C^\dagger C.
    \label{eq:wolf_radon_nikodym_nonempty_family_preliminary_effect_sum}
\end{align}
Define the residual operator
\begin{align}
    R&=\Id_r-C^\dagger C.
    \label{eq:wolf_radon_nikodym_nonempty_family_residual_definition}
\end{align}
By (\ref{eq:wolf_radon_nikodym_nonempty_family_contraction_bound}), $R$ is
positive. Its Stinespring compression under $V$ vanishes.

Nonemptiness is used exactly once. Choose $i_0\in I$ and set
\begin{align}
    P_{i_0}&=E_{i_0}+R,
    \label{eq:wolf_radon_nikodym_nonempty_family_corrected_effect}\\
    P_i&=E_i\qquad(i\neq i_0).
    \label{eq:wolf_radon_nikodym_nonempty_family_unchanged_effects}
\end{align}
Then
\begin{align}
    \sum_{i\in I}P_i
        &=\sum_{i\in I}E_i+R
    \label{eq:wolf_radon_nikodym_nonempty_family_completed_sum_first}\\
        &=C^\dagger C+\Id_r-C^\dagger C
    \label{eq:wolf_radon_nikodym_nonempty_family_completed_sum_second}\\
        &=\Id_r.
    \label{eq:wolf_radon_nikodym_nonempty_family_completed_resolution}
\end{align}
Because the compression of $R$ vanishes, the adjustment does not change the
component map assigned to $i_0$. This construction completes the identity
resolution even when the supplied dilation is nonminimal.

If $d'=0$, all source matrices vanish. The separate formal branch assigns
$\Id_r$ to one chosen outcome and zero to every other outcome. The square
declaration is obtained by specializing the rectangular theorem.

\section{Verdict}

The nonempty case has no mathematical proof gap. The nonempty-family condition
is a local correction to the well-posedness boundary of the printed
finite-family statement in Ref.~\cite{Wolf2012Quantum}. An empty-index theorem
would require a different conclusion, such as a restriction to zero dilation
space or the omission of the identity resolution. Such a variant is not the
instrument theorem used in the development.
