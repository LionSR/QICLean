\section{The assertion}

Proposition~2.11 of Ref.~\cite{Wolf2012Quantum} states that every qubit
channel $T:\MN{2}\to\MN{2}$ admits invertible completely positive maps
$\Phi_1$ and $\Phi_2$, each of Kraus rank one, such that
\begin{align}
    T'
        &=\Phi_2\circ T\circ\Phi_1
    \label{eq:wolf_prop2_11_lorentz_scalar_filtering_gap_filtered_channel}
\end{align}
is a qubit channel in one of three Lorentz normal forms: diagonal,
non-diagonal, or singular.\footnote{Local source:
\path{Notes/WolfNoteTexSource/ch02_representations.tex}, lines~1021--1035.}
Each filtering is represented by
\begin{align}
    \Phi_X(A)
        &=XAX^\dagger,
    \label{eq:wolf_prop2_11_lorentz_scalar_filtering_gap_general_filter}\\
    X
        &\in\operatorname{GL}(2,\C).
    \label{eq:wolf_prop2_11_lorentz_scalar_filtering_gap_invertible_matrix}
\end{align}
The source therefore permits all invertible Kraus-rank-one completely positive
filterings, not only those represented by determinant-one matrices.

\section{Determinant-one action and scalar freedom}

Choose $c\in\C$ such that $c^2=\det X$ and set $S=c^{-1}X$. Then
\begin{align}
    X
        &=cS,
    \label{eq:wolf_prop2_11_lorentz_scalar_filtering_gap_matrix_factorization}\\
    S
        &\in\operatorname{SL}(2,\C),
    \label{eq:wolf_prop2_11_lorentz_scalar_filtering_gap_special_linear_factor}\\
    \Phi_X(A)
        &=|c|^2\Phi_S(A),
    \label{eq:wolf_prop2_11_lorentz_scalar_filtering_gap_filter_factorization}\\
    |c|^2
        &=|\det X|.
    \label{eq:wolf_prop2_11_lorentz_scalar_filtering_gap_scalar_determinant}
\end{align}
For two filters $X_i=c_iS_i$, this gives
\begin{align}
    \Phi_{X_2}\circ T\circ\Phi_{X_1}
        &=|c_1c_2|^2
          (\Phi_{S_2}\circ T\circ\Phi_{S_1}).
    \label{eq:wolf_prop2_11_lorentz_scalar_filtering_gap_two_filter_scalar}
\end{align}
The matrices $S_1$ and $S_2$ determine the determinant-one Lorentz action.
The positive scalar $|c_1c_2|^2$ selects a normalization on the resulting ray
of completely positive maps.

This scalar is irrelevant when one considers only a Lorentz orbit up to
positive scale. It is necessary when the representative must be a channel.
Suppose the determinant-one representative obeys
\begin{align}
    \tr\!\left((\Phi_{S_2}\circ T\circ\Phi_{S_1})(A)\right)
        &=\alpha\,\tr(A).
    \label{eq:wolf_prop2_11_lorentz_scalar_filtering_gap_trace_scalar}
\end{align}
It is trace preserving exactly when $\alpha=1$. General invertible filters can
multiply the representative by $\alpha^{-1}$ through
(\ref{eq:wolf_prop2_11_lorentz_scalar_filtering_gap_two_filter_scalar});
determinant-one filters cannot. The minimization in Propositions~2.8 and~2.9
of Ref.~\cite{Wolf2012Quantum} makes the two partial traces proportional to
the identity, but a further scalar choice is required to make the qubit
representative trace preserving.

\section{The point at issue}

A former local theorem required determinant-one matrices for both filters and
simultaneously required the filtered map to be a qubit channel in one of the
three canonical forms.\footnote{The removed declaration was
\leanid{Wolf.exists_lorentz_normal_form_qubit} in

(\ref{eq:wolf_prop2_11_lorentz_scalar_filtering_gap_two_filter_scalar}) shows
that this determinant-one hypothesis does not provide the normalization used
in Proposition~2.11 of Ref.~\cite{Wolf2012Quantum}. The local theorem was
therefore removed rather than presented as a formalization of the source
proposition.

\section{Canonical representatives and parameter conditions}

The three displayed representatives are formalized from Theorem~8 and
Equations~(17)--(19) of
Ref.~\cite{VerstraeteVerschelde2002QuantumChannels}. That source defines
$R_\Phi$ from the first-factor-partially-transposed dual state and then uses
\begin{align}
    \begin{pmatrix}1\\x'\end{pmatrix}
        &=R_\Phi
          \begin{pmatrix}1\\x\end{pmatrix}.
    \label{eq:wolf_prop2_11_lorentz_scalar_filtering_gap_bloch_action}
\end{align}
Thus $R_\Phi$ is the Bloch transfer matrix and corresponds to the local
$\widehat T$, not to the raw Pauli correlation matrix of the Choi matrix
$\tau$. In the normalized Choi convention and the local tensor-factor order,
\begin{align}
    R_{\mathrm{raw}}(\tau)
        &=\widehat T\operatorname{diag}(1,1,-1,1),
    \label{eq:wolf_prop2_11_lorentz_scalar_filtering_gap_partial_transpose_bridge}\\
    \sigma_2^{\mathsf T}
        &=-\sigma_2.
    \label{eq:wolf_prop2_11_lorentz_scalar_filtering_gap_pauli_two_transpose}
\end{align}
The partial-transpose sign is therefore absorbed before the parameters in
Theorem~8 of Ref.~\cite{VerstraeteVerschelde2002QuantumChannels} are
introduced.

For the diagonal representative, set $q_i=p_i^2$ in the Pauli family of
Equation~(18) of Ref.~\cite{VerstraeteVerschelde2002QuantumChannels} and
specialize to $A=B=\one$. Direct Pauli conjugation gives
\begin{align}
    s_1
        &=q_0+q_1-q_2-q_3,
    \label{eq:wolf_prop2_11_lorentz_scalar_filtering_gap_diagonal_s1}\\
    s_2
        &=q_0-q_1+q_2-q_3,
    \label{eq:wolf_prop2_11_lorentz_scalar_filtering_gap_diagonal_s2}\\
    s_3
        &=q_0-q_1-q_2+q_3,
    \label{eq:wolf_prop2_11_lorentz_scalar_filtering_gap_diagonal_s3}\\
    1
        &=q_0+q_1+q_2+q_3.
    \label{eq:wolf_prop2_11_lorentz_scalar_filtering_gap_weight_sum}
\end{align}
In Wolf's Pauli-transfer convention, inversion yields
\begin{align}
    q_0
        &=\frac{1+s_1+s_2+s_3}{4},
    \label{eq:wolf_prop2_11_lorentz_scalar_filtering_gap_weight_q0}\\
    q_1
        &=\frac{1+s_1-s_2-s_3}{4},
    \label{eq:wolf_prop2_11_lorentz_scalar_filtering_gap_weight_q1}\\
    q_2
        &=\frac{1-s_1+s_2-s_3}{4},
    \label{eq:wolf_prop2_11_lorentz_scalar_filtering_gap_weight_q2}\\
    q_3
        &=\frac{1-s_1-s_2+s_3}{4}.
    \label{eq:wolf_prop2_11_lorentz_scalar_filtering_gap_weight_q3}
\end{align}

Theorem~8 of Ref.~\cite{VerstraeteVerschelde2002QuantumChannels} instead
prints
\begin{align}
    1-s_1-s_2-s_3
        &\geq0.
    \label{eq:wolf_prop2_11_lorentz_scalar_filtering_gap_printed_all_minus}
\end{align}
This condition is inconsistent with Equation~(16) of the same source: the
identity channel has
\begin{align}
    R_\Phi
        &=\operatorname{diag}(1,1,1,1)
    \label{eq:wolf_prop2_11_lorentz_scalar_filtering_gap_identity_transfer}
\end{align}
and violates
(\ref{eq:wolf_prop2_11_lorentz_scalar_filtering_gap_printed_all_minus}).
The discrepancy cannot be explained by the partial-transpose bridge because
$R_\Phi$ is defined after that transpose and already acts as the Bloch
transfer matrix. The formalization records the all-minus condition as a
printed sign inconsistency and does not use it.

The formal development proves that the weights sum to one and that their
simultaneous nonnegativity is equivalent to nonnegativity of the four
numerators in
(\ref{eq:wolf_prop2_11_lorentz_scalar_filtering_gap_weight_q0})--%
(\ref{eq:wolf_prop2_11_lorentz_scalar_filtering_gap_weight_q3}). Under this
nonnegativity assumption, the amplitudes in Equation~(18) of
Ref.~\cite{VerstraeteVerschelde2002QuantumChannels} are
\begin{align}
    p_i
        &=\sqrt{q_i},
    \label{eq:wolf_prop2_11_lorentz_scalar_filtering_gap_pauli_amplitudes}\\
    A
        &=B=\one.
    \label{eq:wolf_prop2_11_lorentz_scalar_filtering_gap_identity_filters}
\end{align}
The resulting channel has
\begin{align}
    \widehat T
        &=\operatorname{diag}(1,s_1,s_2,s_3),
    \label{eq:wolf_prop2_11_lorentz_scalar_filtering_gap_diagonal_transfer}\\
    \operatorname{rank}_{\mathrm{Choi}}(T)
        &=\#\{i\mid q_i\neq0\}.
    \label{eq:wolf_prop2_11_lorentz_scalar_filtering_gap_diagonal_rank}
\end{align}
This formula retains every rank-drop boundary. No necessity theorem for
complete positivity is formalized because the definition uses $\sqrt{q_i}$
and therefore clips a negative candidate weight to zero.

Under the ordering assumed in Equation~(2.40) of
Ref.~\cite{Wolf2012Quantum},
\begin{align}
    1
        &\geq s_1\geq s_2\geq|s_3|,
    \label{eq:wolf_prop2_11_lorentz_scalar_filtering_gap_wolf_ordering}\\
    s_1+s_2
        &\leq1+s_3,
    \label{eq:wolf_prop2_11_lorentz_scalar_filtering_gap_wolf_cp_bound}
\end{align}
all four weight inequalities follow, in agreement with the direct calculation
from Equation~(18) of
Ref.~\cite{VerstraeteVerschelde2002QuantumChannels}.

For each supplied $x\in[0,1]$, the exact non-diagonal three-Kraus family is a
channel with
\begin{align}
    \Delta
        &=\operatorname{diag}
          \left(\frac{x}{\sqrt3},\frac{x}{\sqrt3},\frac13\right),
    \label{eq:wolf_prop2_11_lorentz_scalar_filtering_gap_nondiagonal_delta}\\
    v
        &=\left(0,0,\frac23\right),
    \label{eq:wolf_prop2_11_lorentz_scalar_filtering_gap_nondiagonal_shift}\\
    \operatorname{rank}_{\mathrm{Choi}}(T)
        &=3
        \qquad\text{if }x<1,
    \label{eq:wolf_prop2_11_lorentz_scalar_filtering_gap_nondiagonal_rank_three}\\
    \operatorname{rank}_{\mathrm{Choi}}(T)
        &=2
        \qquad\text{if }x=1.
    \label{eq:wolf_prop2_11_lorentz_scalar_filtering_gap_nondiagonal_rank_two}
\end{align}
The singular two-Kraus family from Equation~(17) of
Ref.~\cite{VerstraeteVerschelde2002QuantumChannels} is
\begin{align}
    T(X)
        &=\tr(X)\ketbra{0}{0},
    \label{eq:wolf_prop2_11_lorentz_scalar_filtering_gap_singular_map}\\
    \operatorname{rank}_{\mathrm{Choi}}(T)
        &=2.
    \label{eq:wolf_prop2_11_lorentz_scalar_filtering_gap_singular_rank}
\end{align}
The diagonal rank formula follows directly from the Bell family in
Equation~(18) of
Ref.~\cite{VerstraeteVerschelde2002QuantumChannels}. The non-diagonal and
singular rank statements are cases~2 and~3 of Theorem~18 in
Ref.~\cite{WolfCirac2008Dividing}.\footnote{Formal declarations:
\leanid{Wolf.diagonalMap},
\leanid{Wolf.diagonalBellWeight_nonneg_iff},
\leanid{Wolf.pauliTransferMatrix_diagonalMap},
\leanid{Wolf.choiRank_diagonalMap},
\leanid{Wolf.nonDiagonalMap},
\leanid{Wolf.choiRank_nonDiagonalMap_eq_three},
\leanid{Wolf.choiRank_nonDiagonalMap_one},
\leanid{Wolf.singularMap}, and
\leanid{Wolf.choiRank_singularMap} in
\doclink{QICLean/Channel/LorentzNormalForm/CanonicalQubitChannels}.}
These bounded constructions prove sufficiency after $x\in[0,1]$ has been
supplied. They do not derive this range or classify Lorentz orbits.

\section{Elimination plan}

A source-faithful proof requires the following steps.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item[$\checkmark$] General invertible Kraus-rank-one completely positive
    filters are formalized by \leanid{Wolf.InvertibleFilter}. For every
    nonzero dimension, the declaration
    \leanid{Wolf.InvertibleFilter.exists_scalar_slFiltering} gives
    \begin{align}
        X
            &=cS,
        \label{eq:wolf_prop2_11_lorentz_scalar_filtering_gap_general_dimension_factorization}\\
        c
            &\in\C\setminus\{0\},
        \label{eq:wolf_prop2_11_lorentz_scalar_filtering_gap_nonzero_scalar}\\
        c^d
            &=\det X,
        \label{eq:wolf_prop2_11_lorentz_scalar_filtering_gap_general_dimension_root}\\
        S
            &\in\operatorname{SL}(d,\C),
        \label{eq:wolf_prop2_11_lorentz_scalar_filtering_gap_general_dimension_sl}\\
        \Phi_X
            &=|c|^2\Phi_S,
        \label{eq:wolf_prop2_11_lorentz_scalar_filtering_gap_general_dimension_filter}\\
        |c|^2
            &>0.
        \label{eq:wolf_prop2_11_lorentz_scalar_filtering_gap_positive_scalar}
    \end{align}
    The two-filter scalar identity is
    \leanid{Wolf.InvertibleFilter.filteredMap_eq_normSq_smul}.

    \item Apply the minimization argument of Propositions~2.8 and~2.9 in
    Ref.~\cite{Wolf2012Quantum} to the full-Kraus-rank case while retaining
    the proportionality constants of both marginals.

    \item[$\checkmark$] The Hermitian congruence
    \begin{align}
        M
            &\longmapsto SMS^\dagger,
            \qquad S\in\operatorname{SL}(2,\C),
        \label{eq:wolf_prop2_11_lorentz_scalar_filtering_gap_spinor_congruence}
    \end{align}
    is identified with the real Minkowski transformation
    \leanid{Wolf.spinorMatrix}. Its image satisfies the three defining
    conditions of $\operatorname{SO}^{+}(1,3)$, and two-sided filtering acts
    on transfer matrices as $L_2\widehat T L_1$.

    \item[$\checkmark$] The spinor homomorphism onto
    $\operatorname{SO}^{+}(1,3)$ is surjective and has the exact fibres
    $\{S,-S\}$. The boost and rotation exponential identities from
    Equation~(2.44) of Ref.~\cite{Wolf2012Quantum} are formalized. Hence
    arbitrary source Lorentz factors can be lifted through the proved spinor
    cover without extrapolating from the $\operatorname{SU}(2)$ rotation
    case.

    \item Classify the diagonal, non-diagonal, and singular Lorentz orbits
    using the source decomposition and its signed canonical-pair data.

    \item Choose scalar factors that make each canonical representative trace
    preserving, and derive the parameter bounds from the positive two-qubit
    normal forms. Once the Bell-diagonal eigenvalues are nonnegative and
    $x\in[0,1]$ is supplied, the exact Kraus constructions, complete
    positivity, transfer data, and rank calculations are already formalized.

    \item Prove the three-case existence theorem with general invertible
    filters and connect it to the existing canonical-form predicates.
\end{enumerate}

The discrepancy remains open. The canonical predicates, exact representatives
and ranks, square full-Kraus-rank minimization theorem, determinant-one
Lorentz action, and full spinor cover are available. The scalar-normalized
Lorentz-orbit classification and the necessity of the parameter range in
Proposition~2.11 of Ref.~\cite{Wolf2012Quantum} have not been formalized.
