\section{A finite-measure obstruction in Theorem 5.8}

Wolf's Theorem~5.8, Equation~(5.18) \cite{Wolf2012Quantum}, is the source assertion.
The source PDF, \path{Notes/WolfNotePDF/ch05_operator_inequalities.pdf},
page numbered 79, contains both the displayed kernel and the words
``positive finite measure.'' Thus this is not a transcription defect.
The assertion is that operator convexity on $[0,\infty)$ is equivalent to
\begin{align}
 f(x)&=a+bx+cx^2+\int_0^\infty\frac{yx^2}{y+x}\,d\mu(y),
 \label{eq:wolf_ch5_finite_measure_representation_source}
\end{align}
where $a,b\in\mathbb R$, $c\geq0$ and $\mu$ is a finite positive measure.
The finite-measure condition in Equation~(5.18), with its displayed kernel,
cannot characterize all operator-convex functions on the closed positive
half-line. Indeed, put $M=\mu((0,\infty))<\infty$. For $x>0$ and $y>0$,
\begin{align}
  0&\leq \frac{yx^2}{y+x}\leq x^2.
  \label{eq:wolf_ch5_finite_measure_representation_kernel_bound}
\end{align}
Integrating Equation~\ref{eq:wolf_ch5_finite_measure_representation_kernel_bound}
shows that every function with the displayed representation satisfies
\begin{align}
  0&\leq f(x)-a-bx\leq (c+M)x^2.
  \label{eq:wolf_ch5_finite_measure_representation_tangent_remainder}
\end{align}
In particular $f(0)=a$, its right derivative at zero exists and equals $b$,
and its remainder after subtracting that tangent is bounded by a constant
times $x^2$ near zero.

Take $f(x)=x^{3/2}$. This function is operator convex on $[0,\infty)$
(the power-convexity result already covers this exponent), but it cannot
have the displayed finite-measure representation. Evaluation at zero gives
$a=0$. Dividing Equation~\ref{eq:wolf_ch5_finite_measure_representation_tangent_remainder} by $x$ and letting $x\downarrow0$ gives $b=0$.
Equation~\ref{eq:wolf_ch5_finite_measure_representation_tangent_remainder} would then give $x^{-1/2}\leq c+M$ for every $x>0$, a
contradiction. This obstruction concerns the representation itself, not
merely the proof of the Jensen consequences.

A corrected characterization must change the measure condition or the
kernel and normalization, and must specify the endpoint regularity. Merely
dropping finiteness without specifying convergence is not a replacement
theorem. The general integral characterization remains open pending that
precise replacement. Its necessity direction must not be used as a
prerequisite for arbitrary-function Jensen in its present form. The
sufficiency direction with a finite positive measure is not contradicted
by this example.


\section{Verdict}
The necessity direction of the printed characterization is false. The
counterexample is a mathematical argument, not a formalized counterexample.
The classification remains open because a corrected replacement
characterization, with precise convergence and endpoint hypotheses, has not
been supplied. This is distinct from the open general formalization scope
recorded in \path{docs/paper-gaps/wolf_ch5_operator_convexity_scope.tex}.
