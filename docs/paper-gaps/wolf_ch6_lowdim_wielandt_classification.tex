\section{The assertion}

For a Kraus family on $\C^d$, let $\mathcal K_1\subseteq\MN{d}$ denote its
one-step linear span. The corollary following Wolf's quantum Wielandt
inequality states that, when $d=2$ or $d=3$, every primitive quantum channel
has an element of $\mathcal K_1$ with a nonzero eigenvalue. It then concludes
that the channel's Wielandt index is at most $d^2$
\cite[Chapter~6, lines~1110--1126]{Wolf2012Quantum}. The same
low-dimensional observation appears after Theorem~1 of
Ref.~\cite{Sanz2010Quantum}.

The proof in Ref.~\cite{Wolf2012Quantum} invokes a classification of
nilpotent matrix spaces but does not prove that classification or resolve the
bibliography placeholders in the local source. In dimension two, the required
claim is
\begin{align}
    \mathcal N\subseteq\MN{2}\text{ linear and nilpotent}
        \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
        \dim\mathcal N\leq1.
    \label{eq:wolf_ch6_lowdim_wielandt_classification_dim_two_bound}
\end{align}
In dimension three, Wolf describes two similarity types for nilpotent spaces
of dimension greater than one: the strictly upper-triangular space and an
exceptional two-dimensional space \cite[Chapter~6]{Wolf2012Quantum}. The
strictly upper-triangular type is incompatible with trace preservation, while
the exceptional type yields a channel with degenerate peripheral spectral
radius \cite[Chapter~6]{Wolf2012Quantum}. The lecture notes do not contain the
classification or the two obstruction arguments.

Fasoli's classification of nilpotent linear spaces is a relevant external
source \cite{Fasoli1997Classification}. The exact chain of results required
for Wolf's low-dimensional corollary must nevertheless be reconstructed and
checked.

exceptional similarity type and the proof that this type obstructs

\section{The dimension-three classification}

Let $\mathcal N\subseteq\MN{3}$ be a linear space of nilpotent matrices without
a common nonzero kernel vector. By the square-zero branch it contains an
element $A$ with $A^2\neq0$, and $A$ is similar to the cyclic shift
$J=E_{12}+E_{23}$ through the basis $(A^2v,Av,v)$. Polarizing
$\operatorname{tr}((X\pm C)^k)=0$ for $k=2,3$ gives
\begin{align*}
    \operatorname{tr}(XC)=0,\qquad\operatorname{tr}(X^2C)=0
    \qquad(X,C\in\mathcal N).
\end{align*}
Since $\ker J=\operatorname{span}\{e_1\}$, some $B\in\mathcal N$ has
$Be_1\neq0$. The trace identities against $J$ force $c=B_{32}\neq0$ and
determine $B$ from $(c,B_{11},B_{23})$; conjugation by the unitriangular
matrix $S=1+\alpha J+\beta J^2$ with $\alpha=-B_{11}/c$ and
$\beta=(cB_{23}+2B_{11}^2)/c^2$, which commutes with $J$, takes $c^{-1}B$
to $B_0=E_{32}-E_{21}$. In these coordinates the trace identities of an
arbitrary member $C$ against $J$, $B_0$ and $J+B_0$, together with
$\operatorname{tr}C=\operatorname{tr}C^2=0$, give $C\in\operatorname{span}\{J,B_0\}$.
This is Wolf's exceptional two-dimensional type. Every member maps
$\operatorname{span}\{e_2\}$ into $\operatorname{span}\{e_1,e_3\}$ and back,
so with $P_0$ and $P_1=1-P_0$ the corresponding (conjugated) coordinate
projections, every member satisfies $MP_0=P_1M$.

For a trace-preserving Kraus family with this one-step span,
$\sum_iK_i^\dagger(P_0-P_1)K_i=(\sum_iK_i^\dagger K_i)(P_1-P_0)=-(P_0-P_1)$,
so $-1$ is an eigenvalue of the trace adjoint and hence of the channel. The
channel is therefore not primitive. This proves Wolf's assertion that the
exceptional type only leads to channels with a degenerate peripheral
spectrum, without the general classification of nilpotent spaces.

\section{Formalization}

The dimension-three statements are
\leanid{QICLean.exists_grading_of_isNilpotent_submodule_fin_three},
\leanid{Kraus.not_isPrimitive_of_isTP_of_forall_isNilpotent_fin_three} and
\leanid{Kraus.exists_not_isNilpotent_mem_wordSpan_one_fin_three}. A
non-nilpotent complex matrix has a nonzero eigenvalue,
\leanid{Kraus.exists_nonzero_eigenvector_of_not_isNilpotent}. Combined with
\leanid{Kraus.wielandtIndex_le_of_mem_wordSpan_one_of_nonzero_eigenvalue}
this gives \leanid{Kraus.wielandtIndex_le_four_fin_two} and
\leanid{Kraus.wielandtIndex_le_nine_fin_three}, the bound $q\le d^2$ for
irreducible primitive channels in dimensions two and three. The
classification of nilpotent spaces with a common kernel is not needed and is
not formalized.
