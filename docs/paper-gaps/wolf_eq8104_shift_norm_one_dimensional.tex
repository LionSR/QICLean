\section{Source passage}

Fix an integer $D\geq 1$. Let $N\in\MN{D}$ be the strict upper shift, defined
by
\begin{align}
    N_{i,j}
        &=
        \begin{cases}
            1, & j=i+1,\\
            0, & \text{otherwise}.
        \end{cases}
    \label{eq:wolf_eq8104_shift_norm_one_dimensional_shift_definition}
\end{align}
For $\lambda\in\C$, define the Jordan block
$J_D(\lambda)=\lambda\Id+N$, and let $\|\cdot\|_{\infty}$ denote the largest
singular value.

Equation~(8.104) in Chapter~8 of Ref.~\cite{Wolf2012Quantum} states that, for
every $n\in\mathbb N$ and every natural number
$k_0\leq\min\{n,D-1\}$,
\begin{align}
    |\lambda|^{\,n-k_0}\binom{n}{k_0}
        &\leq \bigl\|J_D(\lambda)^n\bigr\|_{\infty},
    \label{eq:wolf_eq8104_shift_norm_one_dimensional_source_lower_bound}\\
    \bigl\|J_D(\lambda)^n\bigr\|_{\infty}
        &\leq
        \sum_{k=0}^{\min\{n,D-1\}}
            |\lambda|^{\,n-k}\binom{n}{k}.
    \label{eq:wolf_eq8104_shift_norm_one_dimensional_source_upper_bound}
\end{align}
The local source derives these bounds at lines~1197--1215. The upper bound
follows from the binomial expansion
\begin{align}
    J_D(\lambda)^n
        &=
        \sum_{k=0}^{\min\{n,D-1\}}
            \binom{n}{k}\lambda^{n-k}N^k
    \label{eq:wolf_eq8104_shift_norm_one_dimensional_binomial_expansion}
\end{align}
and the triangle inequality. At line~1215, the source removes the shift
factors by asserting that $\|N\|_{\infty}=1$.

\section{The deviation}

The asserted equality holds when $D\geq 2$, but not when $D=1$. In one
dimension, the strict upper shift defined in
(\ref{eq:wolf_eq8104_shift_norm_one_dimensional_shift_definition}) is the zero
matrix. Consequently,
\begin{align}
    D=1
        \quad&\Longrightarrow\quad
        N=0,
    \label{eq:wolf_eq8104_shift_norm_one_dimensional_one_dimensional_shift}\\
    D=1
        \quad&\Longrightarrow\quad
        \|N\|_{\infty}=0.
    \label{eq:wolf_eq8104_shift_norm_one_dimensional_one_dimensional_norm}
\end{align}
The source states $\|N\|_{\infty}=1$ without restricting the dimension. Since
the Jordan block and Equation~(8.104) of Ref.~\cite{Wolf2012Quantum} both
allow $D=1$, this intermediate assertion includes one degenerate case in
which it is false.

\section{Formalized interpretation}

The derivation of the upper bound requires only $\|N\|_{\infty}\leq 1$.
Submultiplicativity gives
\begin{align}
    \|N^k\|_{\infty}
        &\leq \|N\|_{\infty}^{\,k},
    \label{eq:wolf_eq8104_shift_norm_one_dimensional_power_submultiplicativity}\\
    \|N\|_{\infty}\leq 1
        \quad&\Longrightarrow\quad
        \|N^k\|_{\infty}\leq 1.
    \label{eq:wolf_eq8104_shift_norm_one_dimensional_power_bound}
\end{align}
Applying
(\ref{eq:wolf_eq8104_shift_norm_one_dimensional_power_bound}) to the triangle
inequality for
(\ref{eq:wolf_eq8104_shift_norm_one_dimensional_binomial_expansion}) yields
the upper bound
(\ref{eq:wolf_eq8104_shift_norm_one_dimensional_source_upper_bound}).

The formalization therefore proves the dimension-uniform inequality
$\|N\|_{\infty}\leq 1$ and derives the corresponding power bound from
it.\footnote{Formal declarations:
\leanid{Matrix.l2_opNorm_nilpotentShift_le_one} and
\leanid{Matrix.l2_opNorm_nilpotentShift_pow_le_one}.}
The proof follows the route indicated in Ref.~\cite{Wolf2012Quantum}. The Gram
matrix satisfies
\begin{align}
    N^{\dagger}N
        &=\operatorname{diag}(0,1,\ldots,1),
    \label{eq:wolf_eq8104_shift_norm_one_dimensional_gram_identity}\\
    \bigl\|N^{\dagger}N\bigr\|_{\infty}
        &\leq 1.
    \label{eq:wolf_eq8104_shift_norm_one_dimensional_gram_norm_bound}
\end{align}
The first identity is also formalized directly.\footnote{Formal declaration:
\leanid{Matrix.conjTranspose_nilpotentShift_mul_self}.}
The $C^*$-identity then gives
\begin{align}
    \|N\|_{\infty}^{\,2}
        &=\bigl\|N^{\dagger}N\bigr\|_{\infty}
        \leq 1,
    \label{eq:wolf_eq8104_shift_norm_one_dimensional_cstar_norm_bound}\\
    \|N\|_{\infty}
        &\leq 1.
    \label{eq:wolf_eq8104_shift_norm_one_dimensional_shift_norm_bound}
\end{align}

When $D=1$, the hypothesis $k_0\leq\min\{n,D-1\}$ forces $k_0=0$. The Jordan
block is the scalar matrix $J_1(\lambda)=\lambda\Id$, so both sides of
Equation~(8.104) of Ref.~\cite{Wolf2012Quantum} reduce to
\begin{align}
    |\lambda|^n
        &\leq \|\lambda^n\|_{\infty}
        \leq |\lambda|^n.
    \label{eq:wolf_eq8104_shift_norm_one_dimensional_scalar_bounds}
\end{align}
Thus the source estimate remains valid in one dimension despite the false
intermediate equality. The formal lower bound, upper bound, and combined bound
retain Wolf's hypotheses, including $D\geq 1$, without any additional
dimension restriction.\footnote{Formal declarations:
\leanid{Matrix.le_l2_opNorm_jordanBlock_pow},
\leanid{Matrix.l2_opNorm_jordanBlock_pow_le}, and
\leanid{Matrix.l2_opNorm_jordanBlock_pow_bounds}.}

\section{Verdict}

Replacing the source's intermediate equality $\|N\|_{\infty}=1$ by
\begin{align}
    \|N\|_{\infty}
        &\leq 1
    \label{eq:wolf_eq8104_shift_norm_one_dimensional_corrected_shift_statement}
\end{align}
corrects the degenerate case $D=1$ without weakening Equation~(8.104) of
Ref.~\cite{Wolf2012Quantum}. The correction is local and resolved; the
formalized Jordan-block estimate has exactly the source's hypotheses.
