\section{The printed coefficients}
Hastings, \emph{Solving gapped Hamiltonians locally}~\cite{Hastings2006Solving},
\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0508554v3}{arXiv:cond-mat/0508554v3},
source \path{proj3new.tex}, lines 1005--1009, expands a Gaussian-filtered
local term in iterated commutators. For even $k$ the printed coefficient
contains $(q/\Delta E^2)^k$. The normalized Gaussian in the defining
integral (source lines 194--207) has variance $q/\Delta E^2$, so the required
power is $k/2$.

\section{The expansion}
Let $q>0$, $\Delta E>0$, and $H$ be a finite-dimensional Hermitian operator.
Put $\sigma^2=q/\Delta E^2$ and $\operatorname{ad}_H(A)=HA-AH$. If $t$ has
mean-zero Gaussian distribution of variance $\sigma^2$, then
\begin{align}
 \mathbb E(t^{2r})&=(2r-1)!!\,\sigma^{2r}, &
 \mathbb E(t^{2r+1})&=0.
 \label{eq:hastings05_gaussian_moments}
\end{align}
Expanding $e^{itH}Ae^{-itH}$ and using
(\ref{eq:hastings05_gaussian_moments}) gives
\begin{align}
 \widetilde A
 &=\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^r}{2^r r!}
       \left(\frac q{\Delta E^2}\right)^r
       \operatorname{ad}_H^{2r}(A).
 \label{eq:hastings05_gaussian_commutator_expansion}
\end{align}
For the norm convergence, diagonalize $H$. In an orthonormal eigenbasis,
$\operatorname{ad}_H$ multiplies the $(i,j)$ coefficient by $E_i-E_j$.
The Gaussian filter multiplies that coefficient by
$e^{-\sigma^2(E_i-E_j)^2/2}$, so it is precisely
$\exp[-(\sigma^2/2)\operatorname{ad}_H^2]$. The norm-convergent
exponential series gives (\ref{eq:hastings05_gaussian_commutator_expansion})
without interchanging an integral and a series. Thus, in the convention
$\widetilde A=A+\sum_{k\ge1}a_k i^k\operatorname{ad}_H^k(A)$, the coefficients
are $a_k=0$ for odd $k$. For even $k\ge2$, they are
\begin{align}
 a_k&=\frac{(k-1)!!}{k!}\left(\frac q{\Delta E^2}\right)^{k/2}.
 \label{eq:hastings05_gaussian_series_coefficients}
\end{align}
In (\ref{eq:hastings05_gaussian_series_coefficients}), the sign
$(-1)^{k/2}$ is already supplied by $i^k$. In a convention without
this factor it belongs in the coefficient instead.

\section{Scope}
The variational lower bound holds for every finite real choice of the
commutator coefficients and is unaffected by this correction. The corrected infinite
Gaussian expansion is formalized in QICLean independently of the
finite-polynomial variational theorem. The variance parameter may be zero;
no spectral-gap hypothesis is required.
