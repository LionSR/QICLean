\gapnote{local-correction}{resolved}

Landau, Vazirani and Vidick~\cite{Landau2013PolynomialTime} bound the number
of elementary tensors needed to express an ordered word of local operators
across a chain cut. In the proof of Claim~\texttt{c:errorreduction}, the
crossing two-site operator is assigned $d^2$ tensor terms. Its factors must
be indexed by the same pair of indices.\footnote{Source version:
\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/1307.5143v1}{arXiv:1307.5143v1},
\path{algo53.tex}, line~584, immediately before Equation~\texttt{e:decomp}
(lines~585--588). The definition $P_i=1-H_i$ occurs at line~606;
the ordered words are defined in Equation~\texttt{eq:a-exp}, lines~607--609.}
This corrects the indices without changing the stated $d^{2k}$ bound for a
word containing $k$ crossing occurrences.

\paragraph{The printed indices.}
For $d\ge1$, let $E_1,\ldots,E_d$ and $F_1,\ldots,F_d$ be separately
indexed operator families. The expression printed in the source factors as
\begin{align}
    \sum_{j,k=1}^{d}E_j\otimes F_k
        &=\left(\sum_{j=1}^{d}E_j\right)
            \otimes\left(\sum_{k=1}^{d}F_k\right).
    \label{eq:lvv13_independent_indices}
\end{align}
It therefore has operator Schmidt rank at most one. Here operator Schmidt
rank means the least number of elementary tensors in a sum representing the
operator. A general two-site operator need not satisfy this restriction.

For example, on $\C^2$ put $D_a=\ketbra{a}{a}$ for $a\in\{0,1\}$. The
positive contraction
\begin{align}
    P&=D_0\otimes D_0+D_1\otimes D_1
      =\ketbra{00}{00}+\ketbra{11}{11}
    \label{eq:lvv13_projection_example}
\end{align}
has operator Schmidt rank exactly two. The two displayed terms give the
upper bound. For the lower bound, its two diagonal blocks on the first site
are $D_0$ and $D_1$, which are linearly independent. If $P=A\otimes B$,
both blocks would be scalar multiples of $B$, a contradiction.

This example occurs with the local positivity and gap conditions used in
Ref.~\cite{Landau2013PolynomialTime}.\footnote{The local-Hamiltonian
setting is stated in \path{algo53.tex}, lines~209 and~283.}
Take three qubits, put $h_1=\Id-P$ on sites $1,2$ and
$h_2=\Id-D_0\otimes D_0$ on sites $2,3$, and set
$H=h_1\otimes\Id+\Id\otimes h_2$. The two local terms are commuting
positive contractions. For $a,b,c\in\{0,1\}$, their sum acts by
\begin{align}
    H\ket{abc}
      &=\bigl((1-\delta_{ab})+(1-\delta_{b0}\delta_{c0})\bigr)\ket{abc}.
    \label{eq:lvv13_three_site_spectrum}
\end{align}
The energy vanishes only on $\ket{000}$, all other energies are at least
one, and $\ket{001}$ has energy one. Thus the ground state is unique and
the gap is one, while $P_1=\Id-h_1=P$ still has operator Schmidt rank two.

\paragraph{The paired decomposition.}
Fix an orthonormal basis $e_0,\ldots,e_{d-1}$ of the left site. For an arbitrary
operator $T$ on $\C^d\otimes\C^d$, define
\begin{align}
    E_{ab}&=\ketbra{e_a}{e_b},\notag\\
    B_{ab}&=(\bra{e_a}\otimes\Id)T(\ket{e_b}\otimes\Id),\notag\\
    T&=\sum_{0\le a,b<d}E_{ab}\otimes B_{ab}.
    \label{eq:lvv13_block_expansion}
\end{align}
The identity follows by comparing matrix entries. It has $d^2$ terms,
with both factors depending on the same pair $(a,b)$; zero terms are
allowed. No positivity or spectral hypothesis is needed.

Consider now an ordered word $W$ of local operators across a fixed cut.
Suppose $k$ occurrences cross the cut, each on one $d$-dimensional site
on either side, and every other occurrence acts on only one side. Insert
(\ref{eq:lvv13_block_expansion}) at each crossing occurrence.
The ordered product then expands into at most $d^{2k}$ elementary tensors,
since $(A\otimes B)(C\otimes D)=AC\otimes BD$. Consequently,
\begin{align}
    \operatorname{OSR}(W)&\le(d^2)^k=d^{2k}.
    \label{eq:lvv13_word_rank}
\end{align}
This counts occurrences, including repetitions, and includes the empty
word with $k=0$. It does not require different letters to commute.\footnote{The general
support-weighted estimate is
\leanid{Entropy.operatorSchmidtRank_cutOperator_list_prod_le}.}
The correction establishes this algebraic step only. The sampled sum,
its spectral approximation, and the algorithmic conclusions of
Ref.~\cite{Landau2013PolynomialTime} are separate assertions.
