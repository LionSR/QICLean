\section{The printed statement}

Landau, Vazirani and Vidick quote the following form of the matrix Bernstein
inequality.\footnote{The source is \cite{Landau2013PolynomialTime}, version~1,
    source file \path{algo53.tex}, Theorem \path{t:mcb} at lines~616--624.}
Let $X_1,\dots,X_\ell$ be independent identically distributed random
$d\times d$ matrices with $\mathbb E\,X_i=X$ and $\|X_i-X\|\le R$, and put
\begin{align}
    \sigma^2 & :=\max\bigl\{\mathbb E\,(X_i-X)(X_i-X)^{*},\;
    \mathbb E\,(X_i-X)^{*}(X_i-X)\bigr\}.
    \label{eq:lvv13_variance_printed}
\end{align}
Then for every $\ell$ and every $t\ge0$,
\begin{align}
    \mathbb P\Bigl(\Bigl\|\frac1\ell\sum_{i=1}^\ell X_i-X\Bigr\|\ge t\Bigr)
     & \le2d\exp\Bigl(-\frac{\ell t^2}{2\sigma^2+2Rt/3}\Bigr).
    \label{eq:lvv13_variance_bound}
\end{align}
The two expectations in (\ref{eq:lvv13_variance_printed}) are positive
semidefinite matrices, and the maximum of two matrices is not defined in the
Loewner order; nor can a matrix stand in the scalar exponent of
(\ref{eq:lvv13_variance_bound}).

\section{The correction}

The source derives (\ref{eq:lvv13_variance_bound}) from Tropp's rectangular
matrix Bernstein inequality~\cite[Theorem~1.6]{Tropp2012User}, whose variance
parameter is the maximum of the operator norms of the two summed second moments.
The intended reading of (\ref{eq:lvv13_variance_printed}) is therefore
\begin{align}
    \sigma^2 & :=\max\bigl\{\bigl\|\mathbb E\,(X_i-X)(X_i-X)^{*}\bigr\|,\;
    \bigl\|\mathbb E\,(X_i-X)^{*}(X_i-X)\bigr\|\bigr\},
    \label{eq:lvv13_variance_corrected}
\end{align}
which does not depend on $i$ by identical distribution. With this reading
(\ref{eq:lvv13_variance_bound}) follows from Tropp's theorem applied to the
centred summands $Z_i=(X_i-X)/\ell$: they satisfy $\|Z_i\|\le R/\ell$, their
variance parameter is $\ell\sigma^2/\ell^2=\sigma^2/\ell$, and
\begin{align}
    \frac{t^2/2}{\sigma^2/\ell+Rt/(3\ell)} & =\frac{\ell t^2}{2\sigma^2+2Rt/3}.
    \notag
\end{align}
The dimension factor of Tropp's theorem is $d+d=2d$. The statement is otherwise
unchanged: the bound $\|X_i-X\|\le R$ is read almost surely, and the number of
samples $\ell$ is positive, as the average $\frac1\ell\sum_iX_i$ requires.
