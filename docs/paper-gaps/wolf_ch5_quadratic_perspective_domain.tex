\section{The printed assertion}
Wolf's Corollary~5.4(1), on printed page~86, asserts joint operator convexity
of $LR^{-\alpha}L$ for $0\leq\alpha\leq1$, on ``positive $L$ and Hermitian
$R$''.\footnote{The PDF is
\path{Notes/WolfNotePDF/ch05_operator_inequalities.pdf}.
The local transcription,
\path{Notes/WolfNoteTexSource/ch05_schwarz_inequalities.tex}, lines 917--933,
already says positive $R$, without recording the discrepancy.}
The proof invokes Theorem~5.14 with $g(R)=R^\alpha$, and that theorem requires
$g(R)$ positive and invertible.

\section{The point at issue}
Hermitian denominators are insufficient even for ordinary inverses. In dimension
one, put $\alpha=1$, $L_1=L_2=1$, $R_1=-1$, $R_2=-2$, and give both points
weight $1/2$. The mixed value is $-2/3$, while the average endpoint value is
$-3/4$. Convexity would require $-2/3\leq-3/4$, which is false.
For nonintegral exponents, real powers of general Hermitian matrices also need
an additional convention.

Even replacing Hermitian by positive semidefinite is insufficient if negative
powers at zero are assigned zero. With $L_1=L_2=R_2=1$ and $R_1=0$,
the mixed value at equal weights is $2$, whereas the average endpoint value is
$1/2$. These are domain issues, independent of noncommutativity.

\section{The corrected statement}
For $R>0$ and arbitrary rectangular $L$, the map
$(L,R)\mapsto L^\dagger R^{-\alpha}L$ is jointly operator convex for every
$\alpha\in[0,1]$. It also satisfies the corresponding finite probability-mixture
inequality.\footnote{The declarations are
\leanid{Matrix.conjTranspose_rpow_neg_mul_convex} and
\leanid{Matrix.sum_conjTranspose_rpow_neg_mul_le}.}
No positivity, Hermitian, or invertibility assumption is imposed on $L$.
For Hermitian $L$, this recovers $LR^{-\alpha}L$.

More generally, an affine numerator $h$ and an operator-concave positive definite
denominator $g$ on a convex domain give an operator-convex map
$X\mapsto h(X)^\dagger g(X)^{-1}h(X)$.\footnote{This is
\leanid{Matrix.convexOn_affine_quadraticPerspective}; the independent-numerator
form is \leanid{Matrix.convexOn_quadraticPerspective}.}
The proof sums positive Schur-complement block matrices, adds the positive
remainder in the denominator block, and takes its Schur complement.
Applying this result to $g(R)=R^\alpha$ proves the corrected corollary.

\section{Disposition}
The printed domain is false; the positive definite denominator restores the
source's proof route. The corrected quadratic result is proved, including all
exponent and mixing endpoints. For $\alpha=0$, singular denominators are also
possible, but no extension for positive $\alpha$ follows from this endpoint.
Theorem~5.14 for general operator convex or operator antitone functions, and
Corollary~5.4(2--3), remain separate open obligations. They are not marked as
proved by this quadratic specialization.
