\section{The source equivalence}

For fixed dimensions $d$ and $d'$, Proposition~3.5 of
Ref.~\cite{Wolf2012Quantum} states
\begin{align}
    &\exists\,T:\MN{d}\longrightarrow\MN{d'}
      \text{ positive and indecomposable}\nonumber\\
    &\qquad\Longleftrightarrow
      \exists\,\rho\in\MN{d}\otimes\MN{d'}:
      \rho\text{ is an entangled density operator and }\rho^{T_1}\geq0.
    \label{eq:wolf_prop3_5_reverse_implication_source_equivalence}
\end{align}
Immediately before that proposition, the source writes a decomposable witness
as
\begin{align}
    W
        &=P_1+P_2^{T_1},
    \label{eq:wolf_prop3_5_reverse_implication_source_decomposition}\\
    P_1
        &\geq0,
    \label{eq:wolf_prop3_5_reverse_implication_first_positive_part}\\
    P_2
        &\geq0.
    \label{eq:wolf_prop3_5_reverse_implication_second_positive_part}
\end{align}
This is Equation~(3.15) of Ref.~\cite{Wolf2012Quantum}. Both the witness
decomposition and the PPT condition use partial transpose on the first factor,
whose dimension is $d$.\footnote{Local source:
\path{Notes/WolfNoteTexSource/ch03_positive_not_completely.tex},
lines~276--303. Tracked by \ghissue{22}.}

The witness proof below uses the normalized form of Theorem~3 in
Ref.~\cite{LewensteinKrausCiracHorodecki2000}. In Wolf's first-factor
convention, define
\begin{align}
    \mathcal D_1
        &:=
        \left\{aP_1+(1-a)P_2^{T_1}:
          0\leq a\leq1,\,
          P_1,P_2\geq0,\,
          \tr(P_1)=\tr(P_2)=1\right\}.
    \label{eq:wolf_prop3_5_reverse_implication_normalized_decomposable_set}
\end{align}
This is the compact convex set of normalized decomposable witnesses used in
the separation argument.

\section{The rectangular formal statement}

For $d,d'\geq1$, the formalized theorem has the same input-to-output
orientation as Proposition~3.5 of Ref.~\cite{Wolf2012Quantum}:
\begin{align}
    &\exists\,T:\MN{d}\longrightarrow\MN{d'}
      \text{ positive and indecomposable}\nonumber\\
    &\qquad\Longleftrightarrow
      \exists\,\rho\in\MN{d}\otimes\MN{d'}:
      \rho\geq0,\,
      \tr(\rho)=1,\,
      \rho^{T_1}\geq0,\,
      \rho\text{ is not separable}.
    \label{eq:wolf_prop3_5_reverse_implication_formal_equivalence}
\end{align}
The fixed nonzero-dimensional theorem is
\leanid{Matrix.exists_isIndecomposablePositiveMap_iff_exists_isPPT_not_isSeparable}.
The declaration
\leanid{Matrix.exists_isIndecomposablePositiveMap_iff_exists_isPPT_not_isSeparable_all_dimensions}
states the same equivalence for arbitrary natural-number dimensions and
handles the zero-dimensional boundary explicitly.\footnote{Formal module:
\doclink{QICLean/Channel/PPTIndecomposable}.}

The implication from a PPT entangled state to an indecomposable positive map
is
\leanid{Matrix.exists_isIndecomposablePositiveMap_of_isPPT_not_isSeparable}.
It applies the rectangular positive-map criterion at level one. If the
detecting map were decomposable, one could write
\begin{align}
    T
        &=T_1+T_2\theta,
    \label{eq:wolf_prop3_5_reverse_implication_decomposable_map}\\
    (T\otimes\operatorname{id})(\rho)
        &=(T_1\otimes\operatorname{id})(\rho)
          +(T_2\otimes\operatorname{id})(\rho^{T_1}),
    \label{eq:wolf_prop3_5_reverse_implication_decomposable_detection}\\
    (T\otimes\operatorname{id})(\rho)
        &\geq0,
    \label{eq:wolf_prop3_5_reverse_implication_decomposable_nonnegative}
\end{align}
contradicting detection.

\section{The reverse implication}

Let $T:\MN{d}\to\MN{d'}$ be positive and indecomposable, where $d,d'\geq1$.
Let $T^*:\MN{d'}\to\MN{d}$ be its trace-pairing adjoint, and define
\begin{align}
    W_0
        &:=\tau(T^*)
          \in\MN{d}\otimes\MN{d'}.
    \label{eq:wolf_prop3_5_reverse_implication_trace_adjoint_choi_witness}
\end{align}
This is the output-first rectangular Choi matrix of $T^*$. Its rows and
columns are indexed by
$\operatorname{Fin}(d)\times\operatorname{Fin}(d')$, in that order.
Therefore its first factor has dimension $d$, as in Equation~(3.15) of
Ref.~\cite{Wolf2012Quantum}; the construction does not exchange the tensor
factors.

The trace adjoint of a positive map is positive. The rectangular Choi
criterion at level one therefore gives
\begin{align}
    \operatorname{Re}\tr\!\left(W_0\ketbra{\psi}{\psi}\right)
        &\geq0
        \qquad\text{if }\operatorname{SR}(\psi)\leq1.
    \label{eq:wolf_prop3_5_reverse_implication_block_positivity}
\end{align}

Indecomposability implies $T\neq0$. Positivity also implies that
$T(\Id_d)=0$ would force $T=0$. Hence $T(\Id_d)$ is a nonzero positive
operator. The rectangular Choi trace identity gives
\begin{align}
    \tr(W_0)
        &=\frac{1}{d'}\tr\!\left(T(\Id_d)\right),
    \label{eq:wolf_prop3_5_reverse_implication_choi_trace_identity}\\
    \tr(W_0)
        &>0.
    \label{eq:wolf_prop3_5_reverse_implication_positive_witness_trace}
\end{align}
Define the normalized witness
\begin{align}
    W
        &:=\frac{W_0}{\tr(W_0)}.
    \label{eq:wolf_prop3_5_reverse_implication_normalized_witness}
\end{align}
It is Hermitian, block-positive, and has trace one.

The trace-adjoint rectangular Choi correspondence identifies
decomposability of $T$ with membership of $W_0$ in the cone
$P_1+P_2^{T_1}$ from Equation~(3.15) of
Ref.~\cite{Wolf2012Quantum}. Positive rescaling preserves this membership.
Since $T$ is indecomposable,
\begin{align}
    W
        &\notin\mathcal D_1.
    \label{eq:wolf_prop3_5_reverse_implication_outside_normalized_set}
\end{align}

Strictly separate $W$ from the compact convex set $\mathcal D_1$ in the real
vector space of complex matrices. Represent the separating real-linear
functional by a Hermitian matrix and subtract a multiple of the trace
functional. Because $W$ and every $D\in\mathcal D_1$ have trace one, there is
a Hermitian matrix $R$ such that
\begin{align}
    \operatorname{Re}\tr(WR)
        &<0,
    \label{eq:wolf_prop3_5_reverse_implication_strict_negative_pairing}\\
    \operatorname{Re}\tr(DR)
        &\geq0
        \qquad\text{for every }D\in\mathcal D_1.
    \label{eq:wolf_prop3_5_reverse_implication_nonnegative_set_pairing}
\end{align}

At the endpoint $a=1$ in
(\ref{eq:wolf_prop3_5_reverse_implication_normalized_decomposable_set}),
the second inequality holds for every positive trace-one matrix $P$. Scaling
extends it to every $P\geq0$, and self-duality of the positive cone gives
\begin{align}
    R
        &\geq0.
    \label{eq:wolf_prop3_5_reverse_implication_separator_positive}
\end{align}
At the endpoint $a=0$, the inequality holds for $Q^{T_1}$ whenever $Q$ is
positive and has trace one. First-factor transpose is self-adjoint for the
real trace pairing:
\begin{align}
    \operatorname{Re}\tr\!\left(Q^{T_1}R\right)
        &=\operatorname{Re}\tr\!\left(QR^{T_1}\right).
    \label{eq:wolf_prop3_5_reverse_implication_transpose_adjointness}
\end{align}
Scaling and self-duality therefore give
\begin{align}
    R^{T_1}
        &\geq0.
    \label{eq:wolf_prop3_5_reverse_implication_separator_ppt}
\end{align}
The transpose remains on the first factor throughout the argument.

The strict inequality
(\ref{eq:wolf_prop3_5_reverse_implication_strict_negative_pairing}) implies
$R\neq0$. Since $R\geq0$,
\begin{align}
    \tr(R)
        &>0.
    \label{eq:wolf_prop3_5_reverse_implication_separator_positive_trace}
\end{align}
Define
\begin{align}
    \rho
        &:=\frac{R}{\tr(R)}.
    \label{eq:wolf_prop3_5_reverse_implication_ppt_density}
\end{align}
Then
\begin{align}
    \rho
        &\geq0,
    \label{eq:wolf_prop3_5_reverse_implication_density_positive}\\
    \tr(\rho)
        &=1,
    \label{eq:wolf_prop3_5_reverse_implication_density_trace}\\
    \rho^{T_1}
        &\geq0.
    \label{eq:wolf_prop3_5_reverse_implication_density_ppt}
\end{align}
Moreover,
\begin{align}
    \operatorname{Re}\tr(W\rho)
        &<0.
    \label{eq:wolf_prop3_5_reverse_implication_density_detected}
\end{align}
Block positivity of $W$ makes its pairing nonnegative on every separable
operator. Thus
(\ref{eq:wolf_prop3_5_reverse_implication_density_detected}) proves that
$\rho$ is entangled.

This proof does not use closedness of the full unbounded cone or define
decomposability by duality. It separates from the compact trace-one set and
derives the two positivity conditions from its endpoints, following the
normalized witness argument of
Ref.~\cite{LewensteinKrausCiracHorodecki2000}.

\section{The zero-dimensional boundary}

If $d=0$, every map with domain $\MN{0}$ is zero. If $d'=0$, every map with
codomain $\MN{0}$ is also zero. In either case the map is decomposable, so no
indecomposable positive map exists.

The corresponding tensor product is empty, and every matrix on it has trace
zero. Hence no density operator exists. Both sides of
(\ref{eq:wolf_prop3_5_reverse_implication_formal_equivalence}) are false.
This proves the arbitrary-dimensional statement without a hidden
nonzero-dimensional assumption.

\section{Verdict}

The scope restriction is resolved. Both implications in Proposition~3.5 of
Ref.~\cite{Wolf2012Quantum} are formalized with independent dimensions $d$
and $d'$, the source orientation $\MN{d}\to\MN{d'}$, the trace-adjoint
rectangular Choi witness on $\C^d\otimes\C^{d'}$, and partial transpose on the
first factor. The fixed nonzero-dimensional equivalence and the explicit
zero-dimensional boundary are proved. No source-level gap remains in this
proposition.
