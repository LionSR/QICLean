\section{Zero-error capacity: scope}
The formal results below concern a restricted one-shot dichotomy.\footnote{See
\leanid{Kraus.zeroError_dichotomy_eventually}, its single-threshold corollary
\leanid{Kraus.zeroError_dichotomy}, and the helper
\leanid{not_hasOneShotZeroErrorBit_of_posDef_outputs} in
\doclink{QICLean/Kraus/Wielandt/ZeroErrorDichotomy}.}
The footnote to \cite[Theorem~2]{Sanz2010Quantum} allows one-shot capacity. The formalization
covers every channel power at or above the full Kraus-word-span threshold
$i(A)$ in the primitive case, with unchanged channel hypotheses. This is
a stronger threshold assumption than the source condition $n\ge q(E)$,
where $q(E)$ is the first positivity-improving power.
The comparison in \cite[Proposition~1]{Sanz2010Quantum} gives
$q(E)\le i(A)$, not equality. The example following Proposition~2 of \cite{Sanz2010Quantum} uses
the three Pauli Kraus operators $\sigma_\alpha/\sqrt{3}$ and has
$q(E)=1<i(A)=2$. Thus even the source-faithful one-shot threshold remains open.
To remove this restriction, define the positivity-improving index and prove
persistence of positive definite outputs from that index, then apply the
one-shot trace-pairing criterion. No equality of the two indices is assumed.
The full-word-span persistence theorem supplies
positive definite outputs at every such power; the strictly positive
trace pairing excludes orthogonal outputs.

The regularized zero-error classical capacity, allowing entangled inputs
across tensor powers, remains outside the formalized definition.
The regularized argument cited in the proof of
\cite[Theorem~2]{Sanz2010Quantum} refers to Cubitt, Chen and Harrow
\cite{Cubitt2009Superactivation}. A future formalization must
define tensor-power coding and prove the corresponding full-rank-output
criterion; the elementary one-shot trace-pairing argument alone must not
be advertised as that stronger result.
