\section{The source statement}

Wolf's proposition ``Extending cp maps from operator systems'' concerns
arbitrary finite-dimensional $C^\ast$-algebras $\mathcal A$ and $\mathcal B$
\cite[lines~614--626]{Wolf2012Quantum}. Let $S\subseteq\mathcal A$ be an
operator system, and let $T:S\to\mathcal B$ be completely positive. The
proposition asserts that there is a completely positive map
$\widetilde T:\mathcal A\to\mathcal B$ satisfying
\begin{align}
    \widetilde T(x)
        &=T(x)
        \qquad(x\in S).
    \label{eq:wolf_ch01_operator_system_extension_matrix_scope_extension_agreement}
\end{align}
The local source is
\path{Notes/WolfNoteTexSource/ch01_deconstructing_quantum.tex}, lines~617--619
for the statement and lines~620--626 for the proof.

Neither $\mathcal A$ nor $\mathcal B$ is assumed to be a full matrix algebra.
By the finite-dimensional structure theorem, a finite-dimensional
$C^\ast$-algebra is a finite direct sum of full matrix algebras:
\begin{align}
    \mathcal A
        &\cong\bigoplus_k M_{d_k}(\C).
    \label{eq:wolf_ch01_operator_system_extension_matrix_scope_fd_cstar_structure}
\end{align}
Thus Wolf's proposition covers all such direct sums, not only the
single-summand case $\mathcal A=M_m(\C)$.

\section{The formalized domain}

The formalized domain has the full matrix-realized generality stated in the
source:
\begin{align}
    \mathcal A
        &=\bigoplus_{k<r}M_{d_k}(\C).
    \label{eq:wolf_ch01_operator_system_extension_matrix_scope_direct_sum_domain}
\end{align}
It is represented literally by the dependent function type
$\forall k,\MN{d_k}$. The predicates
\leanid{Matrix.IsOperatorSystemDirectSum} and
\leanid{Matrix.IsCPOnOperatorSystemDirectSum} define operator systems and
complete positivity at every matrix level block by block. When $r=1$, they
agree verbatim with the single-summand predicates
\leanid{Matrix.IsOperatorSystem} and
\leanid{Matrix.IsCPOnOperatorSystem}.\footnote{Direct-sum definitions:
\doclink{QICLean/Channel/OperatorSystemExtensionDirectSum}. Single-summand
definitions: \doclink{QICLean/Channel/OperatorSystem}.}

For a full matrix codomain $\MN{p}$, the extension theorem produces
\begin{align}
    \widetilde T
        &:\bigoplus_k\MN{d_k}\longrightarrow\MN{p},
    \label{eq:wolf_ch01_operator_system_extension_matrix_scope_matrix_codomain_extension}\\
    \widetilde T|_S
        &=T.
    \label{eq:wolf_ch01_operator_system_extension_matrix_scope_matrix_extension_agreement}
\end{align}
Complete positivity is expressed by the blockwise predicate
\leanid{Matrix.IsCPMapDirectSum}.\footnote{Formal declaration:
\leanid{Matrix.exists_cp_extension_of_operatorSystem_directSum} in
\doclink{QICLean/Channel/OperatorSystemExtensionDirectSum}.}

The proof transports the single-summand extension theorem along the
block-diagonal embedding
\begin{align}
    \bigoplus_k M_{d_k}(\C)
        &\lhook\joinrel\longrightarrow M_M(\C),
    \label{eq:wolf_ch01_operator_system_extension_matrix_scope_block_diagonal_embedding}\\
    M
        &=\sum_k d_k.
    \label{eq:wolf_ch01_operator_system_extension_matrix_scope_total_matrix_dimension}
\end{align}
The embedding is
\leanid{Matrix.directSumEmbedFin}, constructed from
\leanid{Matrix.directSumDiagonalEmbedding} and the canonical reindexing
\leanid{finSigmaFinEquiv}. It is the same block-diagonal construction used for
the block structure in Wolf's Theorem~6.14 \cite{Wolf2012Quantum}.
Complete positivity at every tensor level descends through the embedding
because a block-diagonal matrix is positive semidefinite exactly when each
diagonal block is positive semidefinite.\footnote{Single-summand extension:
\leanid{Matrix.exists_cp_extension_of_operatorSystem} in
\doclink{QICLean/Channel/OperatorSystemExtension}. Tensor-level descent:
\leanid{Matrix.bipartiteSlice_directSumEmbedFinLevel}.}

\section{The formalized codomain}

The codomain may be any concrete unital $*$-subalgebra
\begin{align}
    \mathcal B
        &\subseteq\MN{p}.
    \label{eq:wolf_ch01_operator_system_extension_matrix_scope_concrete_codomain}
\end{align}
Given $T:S\to\mathcal B$, the formal theorem produces
\begin{align}
    \widetilde T
        &:\bigoplus_k\MN{d_k}\longrightarrow\mathcal B,
    \label{eq:wolf_ch01_operator_system_extension_matrix_scope_star_subalgebra_extension}\\
    \widetilde T|_S
        &=T.
    \label{eq:wolf_ch01_operator_system_extension_matrix_scope_star_subalgebra_agreement}
\end{align}
Complete positivity is stated after the canonical inclusion
$\mathcal B\hookrightarrow\MN{p}$, because the direct-sum complete-positivity
predicate uses a full matrix codomain.\footnote{Formal declaration:
\leanid{Matrix.exists_cp_extension_of_operatorSystem_directSum_starSubalgebra}
in \doclink{QICLean/Channel/OperatorSystemExtensionStarSubalgebra}.}

The construction supplies the final step left implicit in Wolf's proof
\cite[lines~620--626]{Wolf2012Quantum}. First, the direct-sum theorem gives an
ambient completely positive extension
\begin{align}
    F
        &:\bigoplus_k\MN{d_k}\longrightarrow\MN{p}.
    \label{eq:wolf_ch01_operator_system_extension_matrix_scope_ambient_extension}
\end{align}
Next, a trace-preserving completely positive conditional expectation
\begin{align}
    E
        &:\MN{p}\longrightarrow\MN{p}
    \label{eq:wolf_ch01_operator_system_extension_matrix_scope_conditional_expectation}
\end{align}
has range in $\mathcal B$ and restricts to the identity on $\mathcal B$.
Therefore $E\circ F$ is completely positive, takes values in $\mathcal B$, and
agrees with $T$ on $S$. Restricting its codomain gives
(\ref{eq:wolf_ch01_operator_system_extension_matrix_scope_star_subalgebra_extension}).\footnote{Formal
declaration:
\leanid{StarSubalgebra.exists_krausCPTP_conditionalExpectation}.}

\section{Verdict}

\emph{Closed for matrix-realized finite-dimensional algebras.} The domain is a
finite direct sum $\bigoplus_{k<r}M_{d_k}(\C)$, and the codomain is a concrete
unital $*$-subalgebra of a full matrix algebra. This is the representation used
in Wolf's displayed proof \cite[lines~620--626]{Wolf2012Quantum}. The formal
theorem does not quantify over an abstract finite-dimensional $C^\ast$-algebra
type without a specified matrix representation and must not be cited as such
an abstract theorem.

This result closes the matrix-algebra scope gap tracked in
\href{https://github.com/LionSR/TNLean/issues/5645}{TNLean issue \#5645}.
Neither the domain nor the codomain is required to be a single full matrix
algebra.
