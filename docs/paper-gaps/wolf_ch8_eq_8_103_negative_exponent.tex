\section{The source statement}

Let $X=\Lambda+N\in M_D(\C)$ be upper triangular, where $\Lambda$ is diagonal
and $N$ is strictly upper triangular. For an arbitrary submultiplicative norm
satisfying $\lVert\Lambda\rVert\leq1$, Equation~(8.103) of
Ref.~\cite{Wolf2012Quantum} states, for every $n\in\mathbb N$, that
\begin{align}
    \lVert X^n\rVert
        &\leq \lVert\Lambda^n\rVert
        +(D-1)n^{D-1}\lVert\Lambda\rVert^{n-D+1}
        \max\bigl\{\lVert N\rVert,\lVert N\rVert^{D-1}\bigr\}.
    \label{eq:wolf_ch8_eq_8_103_negative_exponent_source_bound}
\end{align}
The local source is
\path{Notes/WolfNoteTexSource/ch08_distance_measures.tex},
lines~1189--1210, especially Equations~(8.103) and~(8.105).

\section{The exponent defect}

If $n<D-1$, then
\begin{align}
    n-D+1
        &<0.
    \label{eq:wolf_ch8_eq_8_103_negative_exponent_negative_exponent_condition}
\end{align}
The source specifies no convention for the resulting negative power in
(\ref{eq:wolf_ch8_eq_8_103_negative_exponent_source_bound}). In particular,
when $\Lambda=0$, the factor
\begin{align}
    \lVert\Lambda\rVert^{n-D+1}
        &=0^{n-D+1}
    \label{eq:wolf_ch8_eq_8_103_negative_exponent_zero_negative_power}
\end{align}
is undefined under the usual real-power interpretation. Replacing the
exponent by truncated natural subtraction would change the printed statement.

The derivation from Equation~(8.105) of Ref.~\cite{Wolf2012Quantum} factors
the diagonal norm as
\begin{align}
    \lVert\Lambda\rVert^{n-k}
        &=
        \lVert\Lambda\rVert^{n-(D-1)}
        \lVert\Lambda\rVert^{(D-1)-k}.
    \label{eq:wolf_ch8_eq_8_103_negative_exponent_source_factorization}
\end{align}
Both factors use natural exponents only when
\begin{align}
    D-1
        &\leq n,
    \label{eq:wolf_ch8_eq_8_103_negative_exponent_natural_power_range}\\
    k
        &\leq D-1.
    \label{eq:wolf_ch8_eq_8_103_negative_exponent_nilpotent_word_range}
\end{align}
The source derivation therefore proves the natural-power form of
(\ref{eq:wolf_ch8_eq_8_103_negative_exponent_source_bound}) only in the
range
(\ref{eq:wolf_ch8_eq_8_103_negative_exponent_natural_power_range}).

\section{Formalization scope}

Equation~(8.105) of Ref.~\cite{Wolf2012Quantum}, whose terms satisfy
$k\leq n$, is formalized for every $n\in\mathbb N$. Equation~(8.103) is
formalized with the explicit hypothesis
(\ref{eq:wolf_ch8_eq_8_103_negative_exponent_natural_power_range}). Its
refined constant is
\begin{align}
    (D-1)\binom{n}{D-1},
    \label{eq:wolf_ch8_eq_8_103_negative_exponent_refined_constant}
\end{align}
under the source hypothesis
\begin{align}
    2(D-1)
        &\leq n,
    \label{eq:wolf_ch8_eq_8_103_negative_exponent_refined_range}
\end{align}
which implies
(\ref{eq:wolf_ch8_eq_8_103_negative_exponent_natural_power_range}). These
results retain an arbitrary submultiplicative seminorm; they do not replace it
with a particular matrix norm.

The corresponding declarations are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \leanid{Matrix.wolf_eq_105_seminorm} for the unrestricted
        Equation~(8.105) of Ref.~\cite{Wolf2012Quantum} word bound;

    \item \leanid{Matrix.wolf_eq_103} for the natural-power form under
        $D-1\leq n$;

    \item \leanid{Matrix.wolf_eq_103_refined} for the refined constant under
        $2(D-1)\leq n$.
\end{itemize}

Equation~(8.111) of Ref.~\cite{Wolf2012Quantum} applies Equation~(8.103) to
the Schur form of $\widehat T-\widehat T_\varphi$, with matrix dimension
$D=d^2$ and $\lVert\Lambda\rVert_\infty=\mu$. Under the natural-power
interpretation, it inherits the restriction
\begin{align}
    d^2-1
        &\leq n.
    \label{eq:wolf_ch8_eq_8_103_negative_exponent_channel_coarse_range}
\end{align}
The corresponding coarse and refined Schur-form estimates are
\leanid{Matrix.wolf_eq_111_schur_form} and
\leanid{Matrix.wolf_eq_111_schur_form_refined}.

The declarations
\leanid{Matrix.wolf_eq_111_schur_form_subperipheralModulus} and
\leanid{Matrix.wolf_eq_111_schur_form_subperipheralModulus_refined} use the
shared source-shaped quantity $\mu$ from
\leanid{Module.End.subperipheralModulus} on the same $D$-dimensional
coordinate space as the Schur matrices. They prove
\begin{align}
    \mu
        &\leq1,
    \label{eq:wolf_ch8_eq_8_103_negative_exponent_subperipheral_bound}
\end{align}
including the convention for an empty subperipheral spectrum. They retain the
hypothesis
\begin{align}
    \lVert\Lambda\rVert_\infty
        &=\mu,
    \label{eq:wolf_ch8_eq_8_103_negative_exponent_diagonal_norm_identification}
\end{align}
so they remain independent of a channel realization.

The channel-level declarations
\leanid{IsPositiveMap.exists_wolf_eq_111} and
\leanid{IsPositiveMap.exists_wolf_eq_111_refined} construct a unitary Schur
representation of $\widehat T-\widehat T_\varphi$. They prove that the
diagonal entries are exactly zero and the subperipheral eigenvalues, establish
(\ref{eq:wolf_ch8_eq_8_103_negative_exponent_diagonal_norm_identification}),
and show
\begin{align}
    \lVert N\rVert_\infty
        &\leq\mu+2\sqrt d.
    \label{eq:wolf_ch8_eq_8_103_negative_exponent_nilpotent_norm_bound}
\end{align}
They assume, respectively,
\begin{align}
    d^2-1
        &\leq n,
    \label{eq:wolf_ch8_eq_8_103_negative_exponent_channel_decl_coarse_range}\\
    2(d^2-1)
        &\leq n.
    \label{eq:wolf_ch8_eq_8_103_negative_exponent_channel_decl_refined_range}
\end{align}

When $d=1$, these ranges allow $n=0$. Although the auxiliary identity
\begin{align}
    T^0-T_\varphi^0
        &=(T-T_\varphi)^0
    \label{eq:wolf_ch8_eq_8_103_negative_exponent_boundary_auxiliary_identity}
\end{align}
fails, the final estimate at this boundary is proved directly: its left-hand
side is zero and its right-hand side is one.

No declaration uses an inverse power of $\mu$. The case $\mu=0$ is therefore
included, both when the subperipheral spectrum is empty and when it consists
only of the zero eigenvalue.

\section{Conclusion}

This is a source scope restriction, not a missing formal proof. The formal
statement records exactly the range in which the natural-exponent derivation
printed in Ref.~\cite{Wolf2012Quantum} is meaningful. Removing the restriction
would require an explicit interpretation of negative powers, including the
case $\lVert\Lambda\rVert=0$. Tracking:
\href{https://github.com/LionSR/TNLean/issues/5837}{TNLean issue \#5837}.
