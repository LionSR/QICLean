\paragraph{Source.}
The source is the frustration-free specialization of Theorem~1.1 of the
September~24, 2026 two-dimensional area-law manuscript~\cite{OpenAI2026AreaLaw}:
finite-range terms $0\le h_i\le J\Id$ with $h_i\Omega=0$ and
$H=\sum_ih_i\ge\Delta(\Id-P)$, $P=\lvert\Omega\rangle\langle\Omega\rvert$.
In this case the amplification stage of Section~10 of the manuscript
(Lemma~10.1 and the input (10.2) of Proposition~10.2) can be fed by one sweep
of local filters built from the terms themselves. With positive contractions
$k_i=h_i/J$, so that $k_i\Omega=0$ and $\sum_ik_i\ge g(\Id-P)$ with
$g=\Delta/J$, put $G_i=(\Id-k_i)^{1/2}$, $K_i=k_i^{1/2}$ and $D=G_N\cdots G_1$. The
norm-loss argument of the detectability lemma~\cite{Anshu2016Simple} gives
\begin{equation}
    \|D^t(\Id-P)\|\le\theta^t,\qquad \theta=\bigl(1+g/(d+1)^2\bigr)^{-1/2},
    \label{eq:sweep}
\end{equation}
where $d$ bounds the number of supports meeting a given support. Together with
the exact light cone of a product of $t$ such sweeps, this replaces the
Poisson-clock filtering of (10.3)--(10.4) of the manuscript.

\paragraph{Formal statement.}
The formalization proves \eqref{eq:sweep} only when every $k_i$ is an
orthogonal projection, for which $G_i=\Id-k_i$. It is the detectability lemma
of Anshu, Arad and Vidick in the form already formalized in QICLean, with the
count $d$ of non-commuting partners. The amplification step is
then run with the range projections $\Pi_i$ of the terms $h_i$ in place of the
$k_i$. These satisfy the three properties the amplification step uses of its filters:
\begin{itemize}
    \item $\Pi_i\Omega=0$, since $\Pi_i=h_i^{-1}h_i$ in the functional calculus;
    \item $\Pi_i$ acts on the support of $h_i$;
    \item $\Pi_i\ge h_i/J$ when $0\le h_i\le J\Id$, so that
          $\sum_i\Pi_i\ge g(\Id-P)$ with the same $g=\Delta/J$.
\end{itemize}
Hence the projection case of \eqref{eq:sweep}, applied to the $\Pi_i$, gives
the same contraction factor $\theta$ for a different sweep operator
$D=(\Id-\Pi_N)\cdots(\Id-\Pi_1)$. The light cone of the sweep and the rest of the amplification argument use
only these three properties, so no hypothesis of the frustration-free model
above is added, and the area law of Theorem~1.1 in this specialization is
unaffected.

\paragraph{What remains open.}
The bound \eqref{eq:sweep} for general positive contractions is not
formalized. It is not needed for the area law. Elimination plan: if a later
step needs it, prove the norm-loss argument of~\cite{Anshu2016Simple} directly, with
$\|K_iv_N\|\le u_i+\sum_{j>i,\,D_j\cap D_i\ne\varnothing}u_j$ and the
inequality $(1-\sqrt{1-x})^2\le x$ on $[0,1]$.
