\section{Isometric coordinates for an orthogonal projection}

The restriction of an operator to a projection range can be expressed in
orthonormal coordinates without imposing an ordering on the ambient basis.
This elementary construction is used for the symmetric restrictions in
\cite[Section~8, lines~80--110 and 370--381]{OpenAI2026AreaLaw}.

\begin{theorem}[Isometric coordinates for a projection]
    \label{thm:projection_range_isometry}
    \lean{IsStarProjection.exists_matrix_range_isometry}
    Let $I$ be a finite set and let $P\in\mathbb C^{I\times I}$ satisfy
    $P^*=P$ and $P^2=P$. Then there exist an integer $n\ge0$ and
    $Z\in\mathbb C^{I\times\{0,\ldots,n-1\}}$ such that
    \begin{align*}
        Z^*Z & =I_n, \\
        ZZ^* & =P.
    \end{align*}
    The assertion includes the empty ambient set and the zero projection.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
    Enumerate the ambient coordinates, choose an orthonormal basis of the
    projection range in these coordinates, and transport the resulting
    isometry back to the original index set. The two matrix identities are
    preserved by the coordinate equivalence.
\end{proof}
