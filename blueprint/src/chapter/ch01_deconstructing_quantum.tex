\chapter{Deconstructing Quantum Mechanics}\label{ch:deconstructing_quantum}

This chapter opens the volume with the finite-dimensional groundwork of
quantum mechanics that the later chapters build on, following Wolf's own
opening chapter \cite[Chapter~1]{Wolf2012Quantum}: a state is a density
operator, a positive semidefinite matrix of trace one, and an observable is
a Hermitian matrix whose expectation value in that state is given by the
trace pairing. It first develops the structure of states themselves: the
Schmidt decomposition of a bipartite vector, purifications of a density
operator, and the steering of a purified state's convex decompositions from
a distant system. It then turns to positive maps, showing that positivity
already implies complete positivity once a map's range or its adjoint's
range is commutative, establishing the partial trace over either tensor
factor, and extending a completely positive map from an operator system to
the ambient matrix algebra. It closes with the spectral facts these
constructions rely on: trace-power identities for Hermitian matrices, the
projective geometry of pure states, and Wigner's theorem classifying the
transformations that preserve transition probabilities between them.
