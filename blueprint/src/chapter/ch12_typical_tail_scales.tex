\section{Typical widths for linear and logarithmic cut budgets}
\label{sec:typical_tail_scales}

The polynomial error asserted after the typical-set construction in
\cite[Section~7]{OpenAI2026AreaLaw} follows from the exponential tail estimate
of Lemma~3.1 once the cut budget is at most linear in the scale.

\begin{theorem}[Uniform polynomial decay of the tail expression]
\label{thm:typical_tail_linear_budget}
\lean{Entropy.exists_typical_tail_bound_of_linear_budget}
\leanok
Fix $\theta\ge0$ and $C>0$. There is an integer $N\ge2$, depending only on
these two constants, such that for every integer $n\ge N$ and every
$0<\mathcal B\le Cn$,
\[
2e^{e/2}\exp\!\left(-\frac{n^{3/5}}
 {32\sqrt{(1+\theta)\mathcal B}}\right)\le n^{-100}.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\leanok
The denominator is at most
$32\sqrt{(1+\theta)C}\,n^{1/2}$. Consequently the left-hand side is bounded
by $2e^{e/2}\exp(-cn^{1/10})$, where
$c=(32\sqrt{(1+\theta)C})^{-1}>0$.
Exponential decay dominates every fixed real power; in particular this
expression is at most $n^{-100}$ for all sufficiently large $n$.
The threshold does not depend on the choice of $\mathcal B$.
\end{proof}

This is a numerical implication. Its physical application still requires
both the geometric bound $\mathcal B\le Cn$ and the marginal tail estimate
for the actual ground state. Given those estimates, the normalized-state
results above give positive selected mass at least $1-n^{-100}$ at width
$n^{3/5}$.

The target-region argument in \cite[Section~8]{OpenAI2026AreaLaw}
also allows a fixed logarithmic factor in the cut budget. The following
specialization uses the twelfth logarithmic power.

\begin{theorem}[Exponent bound for a logarithmic cut budget]
\label{thm:typical_tail_log_budget_exponent}
\lean{Entropy.typicalWidth_div_sqrt_log_pow_twelve_budget_ge}
\leanok
Fix $\theta\ge0$, $C>0$, $n>1$, and
$0<\mathcal B\le Cn(\log n)^{12}$. With
$c=(32\sqrt{(1+\theta)C})^{-1}$, one has
\begin{align}
\frac{c n^{1/10}}{(\log n)^6}
&\le \frac{n^{3/5}}{32\sqrt{(1+\theta)\mathcal B}}.
\end{align}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\leanok
Since $n>1$, the logarithm is positive. The budget bound gives
$32\sqrt{(1+\theta)\mathcal B}
\le32\sqrt{(1+\theta)C}\sqrt n(\log n)^6$.
Divide the identity $n^{3/5}=n^{1/10}\sqrt n$ by this positive upper bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Uniform polynomial decay for a logarithmic cut budget]
\label{thm:typical_tail_log_budget}
\lean{Entropy.exists_typical_tail_bound_of_log_pow_twelve_budget}
\leanok
Fix $\theta\ge0$ and $C>0$. There is an integer $N\ge2$, depending only
on these two constants, such that for every integer $n\ge N$ and every
$0<\mathcal B\le Cn(\log n)^{12}$,
\begin{align}
2e^{e/2}\exp\!\left(-\frac{n^{3/5}}
 {32\sqrt{(1+\theta)\mathcal B}}\right)&\le n^{-100}.
\end{align}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\leanok
\uses{thm:typical_tail_log_budget_exponent}
Let $c=(32\sqrt{(1+\theta)C})^{-1}>0$.
Theorem~\ref{thm:typical_tail_log_budget_exponent} bounds the tail expression
by $2e^{e/2}\exp(-cn^{1/10}/(\log n)^6)$.
The logarithmic asymptotic $(\log n)^6=o(n^{1/20})$ gives
$(\log n)^6\le n^{1/20}$ for all sufficiently large $n$.
Consequently the tail expression is at most
$2e^{e/2}\exp(-cn^{1/20})$, which is eventually at most $n^{-100}$.
Choose $N\ge2$ large enough for both comparisons. Neither comparison
depends on $\mathcal B$.
\end{proof}

This numerical statement does not supply a physical cut budget or a
marginal-tail estimate for a Hamiltonian; those estimates are separate
inputs to its application.
