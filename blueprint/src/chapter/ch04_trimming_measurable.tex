\section{Borel dependence of the collective trimming projections}

The input family is allowed to vary over all finite families of physical
vectors. No orthonormality or nonvanishing restriction is imposed. The
threshold and the sequence of cuts are fixed.

\begin{theorem}[Continuity of the original Gram operator]
    \label{thm:continuous_trimming_gram}
    \lean{Entropy.continuous_leftMarginal_stackVector}
    \leanok
    For a fixed number of vectors, the map
    $(u_i)\mapsto\rho_u=\sum_i|u_i\rangle\langle u_i|$ is continuous.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}\leanok
    \uses{thm:trimming_gram_invariance}
    Each matrix entry is a finite sum of products
    $u_i(x)\overline{u_i(y)}$, hence is continuous in the vectors.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Borel measurability of the actual trimming projections]
    \label{thm:measurable_collective_trimming}
    \lean{Entropy.measurable_trimProj,Entropy.measurable_trimStep}
    \leanok
    \uses{def:viable_trimming}
    Fix any real threshold $\xi$, any sequence of regions $(D_j)$, and
    any nonnegative integer $k$. As functions of the entire finite input
    family $(u_i)$, both the literal product $Q_k$ of the first $k$
    collective-trimming projections and the next cut projection $P^{k+1}$
    are Borel measurable. The empty family, zero vectors, and repeated
    or linearly dependent vectors are included.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}\leanok
    \uses{thm:continuous_trimming_gram,thm:trimming_reduced_gram,
        lem:borel_closed_spectral_cutoff}
    Induct on $k$. The initial product is the constant identity.
    If $Q_j$ is Borel measurable, then so is the next cut marginal
    \begin{align}
        \rho_{j,D_j}
        =\operatorname{Tr}_{D_j^c}
          \bigl(U_{D_j}Q_j\rho_u Q_j^*U_{D_j}^*\bigr).
    \end{align}
    Indeed multiplication, adjoint, reindexing and partial trace are
    continuous, and the original Gram operator is continuous in the family.
    The closed spectral cutoff at $\xi^2$ is Borel measurable, so
    $P^{j+1}=1_{[\xi^2,\infty)}(\rho_{j,D_j})$ is measurable.
    Its lift is continuous in the regional matrix. Multiplication by
    $Q_j$ gives the next product and completes the induction.
\end{proof}

These assertions concern the actual successive products in
\cite[Definition \texttt{def:trimming}, lines 648--655]{Arad2016RigorousRG}.
They do not require monotone cuts or positive thresholds. A measurable
sampling construction, its conditional probability law, and measurability
of the final subspace projection require further arguments.
