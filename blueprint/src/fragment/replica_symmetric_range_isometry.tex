\section{Isometric coordinates for simultaneous symmetric copies}
\label{sec:replica_symmetric_range_isometry}

Let $X_0,\ldots,X_4$ be finite coordinate sets for the five one-copy
factors $Q,Y,V,C,R$, respectively. For an integer $k\geq0$, put
\begin{align}
  \mathcal X_k
    &=\left(\prod_{f=0}^4X_f\right)^{\{0,\ldots,k-1\}}.
\end{align}
A permutation $\sigma\in\mathfrak S_k$ acts on these configurations by
\begin{align}
  (\sigma\cdot x)(j,f)&=x(\sigma^{-1}j,f).
\end{align}
Thus the same permutation acts on the copies of every factor,
including $Y$. Let $U_\sigma$ be its permutation matrix and define
\begin{align}
  S_k&=\frac1{k!}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathfrak S_k}U_\sigma.
\end{align}
This is the simultaneous symmetric projection in Sections~5 and~7
of~\cite{OpenAI2026AreaLaw}. The following auxiliary statement
constructs coordinates for its range.

\begin{theorem}[An isometry onto the simultaneous symmetric range]
  \label{thm:replica_symmetric_range_isometry}
  \lean{TensorPower.exists_symmetricSubspace_isometry}
  \leanok
  For any five finite coordinate sets and every integer $k\geq0$,
  there exist an integer $n\geq0$ and a complex matrix
  $J\in\C^{\mathcal X_k\times\{0,\ldots,n-1\}}$ such that
  \begin{align}
    J^*J&=\Id_n,& JJ^*&=S_k.
  \end{align}
  Empty one-copy factors and $n=0$ are allowed.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{thm:symmetric_projection}
  By Theorem~\ref{thm:symmetric_projection}, the actual average $S_k$
  is self-adjoint and idempotent. Enumerate the finite set
  $\mathcal X_k$ to regard it as a matrix on a finite coordinate
  space. An orthogonal projection admits an orthonormal eigenbasis,
  and its eigenvalues belong to $\{0,1\}$. Let $n$ be the number of
  eigenvectors with eigenvalue $1$, and form a matrix whose columns
  are those vectors. Its columns are orthonormal, and their range
  projection is $S_k$. Transporting the rows back to the original
  configuration set gives $J$ and the two asserted identities.
  If the projection has zero range, the column set is empty and
  $n=0$. The construction also applies at $k=0$, when the empty
  configuration is the sole basis index.
\end{proof}
