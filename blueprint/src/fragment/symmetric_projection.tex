\section{The projection obtained by finite-group averaging}
\label{sec:symmetric_projection}

Let $G$ be a finite group, let $X$ be a finite set, and let
$\varphi:G\to\mathrm{Sym}(X)$ be a homomorphism. Write $U_g$ for
its permutation matrix on $\C^X$, and put
\begin{align}
  P&=\frac1{|G|}\sum_{g\in G}U_g.
\end{align}
The finite group is nonempty, so the averaging factor is defined.
This construction supplies the simultaneous symmetric projection
used in Section~7 of~\cite{OpenAI2026AreaLaw}:
for permutations of the copies, the one-copy coordinate set retains
all physical and auxiliary factors, including the middle physical
factor. The statement below is the general projection identity.

\begin{theorem}[The finite-group symmetrizer is an orthogonal projection]
  \label{thm:symmetric_projection}
  \lean{PermutationRepresentation.isStarProjection_symProj}
  \leanok
  For every finite group action by permutations as above,
  \begin{align}
    P^2&=P,& P^*&=P.
  \end{align}
  The coordinate set $X$ may be empty.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  For every vector $v$, the average $Pv$ is fixed by each $U_h$,
  since left multiplication by $h$ permutes the summation indices.
  Averaging a fixed vector leaves it unchanged. Hence $P^2v=Pv$
  for every $v$, proving $P^2=P$. Each permutation matrix satisfies
  $U_g^*=U_{g^{-1}}$, and inversion permutes the elements of $G$.
  Therefore
  \begin{align}
    P^*&=\frac1{|G|}\sum_{g\in G}U_{g^{-1}}=P.
  \end{align}
  Both identities remain valid on the zero-dimensional space
  $\C^\varnothing$.
\end{proof}
