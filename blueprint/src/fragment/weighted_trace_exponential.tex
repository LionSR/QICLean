\section{Weighted traces of commuting exponentials}
\label{sec:weighted_trace_exponential}

The rough estimate in \cite[Section~7, lines 553--555]{Ope26b} combines two
merge moments by Cauchy--Schwarz. The following arithmetic-mean estimate
also gives a polynomial bound when the doubled moments have polynomial
bounds.

\begin{theorem}[Arithmetic-mean bound for weighted exponential traces]
\label{thm:weighted_trace_exponential}
\lean{Matrix.PosSemidef.re_trace_mul_exp_add_le_half_sum}
\leanok
Let $\rho$ be a positive semidefinite complex matrix on a finite coordinate
set, and let $A,B$ be Hermitian matrices on the same set with $AB=BA$.
For every $a\in\R$,
\begin{align}
 \operatorname{Re}\tr\bigl(\rho e^{a(A+B)}\bigr)
 &\le \frac{\operatorname{Re}\tr(\rho e^{2aA})
       +\operatorname{Re}\tr(\rho e^{2aB})}{2}.
 \label{eq:weighted_trace_exp_mean}
\end{align}
The weight need not have trace one or commute with $A$ or $B$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}\leanok
Set $U=e^{aA}$ and $V=e^{aB}$. These matrices are Hermitian and commute,
so positivity of the weighted trace gives
$0\le\operatorname{Re}\tr(\rho(U-V)^2)$.
Expanding the square yields
$2\operatorname{Re}\tr(\rho UV)
 \le\operatorname{Re}\tr(\rho U^2)+\operatorname{Re}\tr(\rho V^2)$.
The identities $UV=e^{a(A+B)}$, $U^2=e^{2aA}$ and $V^2=e^{2aB}$
then give~(\ref{eq:weighted_trace_exp_mean}).
\end{proof}
